# Benchmark rubric

A sample is the Wiki page the DM requests in the prompt, with its `> [!narration]` callout. The council scores each sample from 1 to 5 on each rubric below, in this order. Four of the six judge the callout, the craft this benchmark grades first. `bun run cfcli -- bench alias` copies this file into each grader's brief, and `run.json` records its sha256.

- **Request.** The page is the one the DM requests and meets each stated constraint, such as a bonus or a source work to build on. Each Campaign fact comes from a Backup page the sample cites, and what the page invents fits that Canon.
- **Mechanics.** Rules text reads as valid 2024 rules wording and is priced for its rarity or level. The mechanics act out the fiction, and each property shows in what the table perceives.
- **Callout fit.** The callout fits its slot in person, tense and length. It gives only what the characters perceive, and the outcome and each PC's actions and feelings wait for the Players. Live action stops before any outcome the Players could change.
- **Evidence.** Concrete evidence the table can perceive takes the place of judgement words and rules terms. A first look gives each feature the table can act on, without assuming who holds or meets it.
- **Spoken clarity.** The callout is clear on one hearing, with specific nouns and short clauses a player can interrupt. It brings in at most three names new to the table and gives the speaker's lead-in before each complete line.
- **Voice.** The callout sounds like the DM at the table at the Brennan Lee Mulligan and Matt Mercer bar, with a second-person camera and specific, playable detail. A long sentence resolves in a short one, and the last line gives the table a live choice or stops on the live moment.

Scale: 1 is broken or off-voice and 2 mostly misses. 3 is adequate and 4 is vivid and exact. 5 stands beside Brennan Lee Mulligan or Matt Mercer at the table, with rules a designer would print.
