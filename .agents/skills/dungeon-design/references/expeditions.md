# Expedition scope

Read during dungeon-design step 3 when the Site is too large to run from one page or is intended for repeated expeditions. Keep Site stocking local: a Region's settlements, wilderness travel and political geography remain location-design's work.

## Bound the next expedition

Identify the Party's available goals and how each changes access, knowledge, resources or relationships. Repeated visits need different reasons to return, not a requirement to kill every occupant. Where people live, their claims, trade and obligations remain part of the situation.

Choose the areas needed for the next likely expedition: approaches, goal routes, an alternative route with a different cost or risk, shelter/retreat and the nearest frontier exposed by those choices. Account for shortcuts from the actual Party's capabilities. Fully stock this reachable scope using the main procedure. Describe farther opportunities as leads on the containing Site rather than creating empty linked pages or pretending unfinished areas are complete. Every physical barrier and hazard has a fictional cause and counterplay, including those at the edge of prepared material.

**Done when** the Party can choose a goal, enter, gain something and return using prepared keys, and the next frontier has a concrete approach, sign and reason to explore it.

## Split only at playable boundaries

A parent Site contains the complex's common situation, entrances, connections between zones, shared pressure and return state. Create a child Site when a zone needs its own usable key and procedures. Build each child from the actual Site template and file it in the parent Site's `Locations/` folder with `parent: "[[Containing Site]]"`. Smaller zones remain groups of keys on one Site.

On each cross-Site route, state the destination Site and entry key, travel cost, conditions and return direction. Put reciprocal references at its endpoints. A parent summary links to the child procedures instead of copying their keys. Give shared clocks one authoritative home. Each child states which of its local actions affect a shared clock. Separate local clocks only when they represent different processes, with any interaction specified.

**Done when** each child is a complete playable Site, nesting is represented by links rather than folders, and crossing a boundary preserves route and pressure state.

## Keep a return ledger

Put a concise `Return state` subsection in the relevant Site's Play. Separate the current facts from conditional future responses:

- Known entrances, opened routes and usable retreat/shelter, including any new exposure.
- What the Party learned, took, damaged, depleted or promised, with the relevant area or owner link.
- Surviving occupants' knowledge, resources and next intended action.
- Pressure state and the elapsed-time/action trigger for the next change.
- Unresolved opportunities and the next frontier that available routes reveal.

Seed the ledger only from Canon and the DM's supplied expedition outcomes. Stocking plans can say what will happen if an action occurs. They do not claim the Party already did it. Leave hot and played Sessions unchanged by a Site update.

**Done when** another Agent or the DM can distinguish what is true now from what might change before the next visit.

## Resolve absence causally

For a return visit, compare the previous state with the stated elapsed time and Party actions. Advance existing pressure triggers, then determine each affected occupant's response using what they actually know and can do. For every repair, arrival, loss or changed route, state the actor or process, its motive and resources, the time required and the visible evidence it leaves. Keep a change pending when its prerequisites are unmet.

Preserve durable geography and the route knowledge the Party gained. Dead occupants remain dead unless a sourced or established mechanism explains their return. Replacement occupants have their own origin. Taken rewards remain taken, broken mechanisms remain broken until repaired, and bargains hold or break for an explicit reason. A changed situation should make prior exploration useful even when it brings new danger.

Update only affected keys, the ledger and the next prepared frontier. Present current facts where the DM will use them, retaining links to their owner pages. Keep historical outcomes in their existing records.

**Done when** every difference on return has a traceable cause and timing, unchanged gains survive, and the next expedition offers choices produced by those consequences rather than an arbitrary reset.
