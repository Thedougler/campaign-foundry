# Registers

A register is the feeling the page means to produce at the table. Read only the heading that matches it. Each register lists the facts that cause the feeling. The page states those facts and leaves the feeling to the Players.

## Horror

Build **vulnerability, uncertainty and violation**, in this order:

1. Establish what normal looks like here.
2. Introduce one precise wrongness.
3. Give evidence without the full explanation.
4. Let what it implies grow worse than the first sight of it.
5. Leave the Party able to act while part of the mystery remains unanswered.
6. Reveal only enough to change what the Players fear.

Useful facts:

- something acting out of sight
- familiar things following the wrong rules
- a trace whose cause is worse than the trace
- absence where presence belongs, or presence where none is possible
- a broken boundary of body, home, faith or nature
- credible harm to something the PCs value
- ordinary protections that already failed
- a pattern the Players recognise before they understand it

Threaten certainty, safety, control, relationships, identity and expectation, as well as bodies. Helplessness inside the fiction builds horror. Taking a Player's choices away builds frustration. Give the Party a move at every point.

## Wonder

Build **novelty, coherence and invitation**.

Give the fantastic thing one extraordinary premise and a local logic it keeps. Then add:

- a scale the Party can measure against, and landmarks they recognise
- something visibly happening
- something they can handle, or something curiosity can uncover
- a consequence proving it is more than scenery

The strange detail convinces when it behaves the same way every time.

## Villainy

Build **evidence of character**. The Players judge the villain from it. Show:

- what the villain chooses when cruelty was avoidable
- who pays for their comfort, ambition, creed, vanity or cowardice
- one specific harm with a specific victim or aftermath
- how they treat someone beneath their notice
- the value they claim beside the exception they make for themselves
- the small habit that makes the larger evil personal
- what they truly love, admire, fear or protect
- why a real person might follow them willingly
