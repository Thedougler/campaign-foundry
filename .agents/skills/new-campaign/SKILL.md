# New Campaign

A Campaign is one group of Players moving through a World, and its **Campaign folder** holds both: the World's pages beside the Campaign's own. Create the starting state, with just enough World to begin play. `collab-with-me` and Prep build what play needs next.

Every path below is vault-relative (from `wiki/`) unless it starts with `docs/`, `archive/` or `.agents/`. The Campaign folder is `<campaign-folder>/`, the Campaign's name as a lowercase slug with hyphens between words and punctuation dropped (`Next Wake` → `next-wake/`). It holds:

- the Campaign overview `<campaign-folder>/<campaign-folder>.md`, `campaign-config.md` and `hot.md`;
- the World overview `<campaign-folder>/<world-slug>.md` and the setting pages by kind (`Locations/`, `NPCs/`, `Factions/` …) when this Campaign's setting is new;
- `PCs/` and `Threads/`, then `Sessions/` once play starts;
- the generated `index.md`, and the `log.md` that `cf log` writes.

A page two Campaigns both play is shared: it lives in a top-level kind folder, `<Kind>/<slug>.md`, and a shared World overview sits at the vault root as `<world-slug>.md`. Every page's filename is its title's slug (`docs/wiki-layout.md` **Page names**), and wikilinks resolve by that slug wherever the page lives, because slugs stay unique across the whole vault.

## Settings

Each run founds its Campaign on one of three settings, which step 1 settles:

- **New.** No World in the vault fits. Step 3 builds a World skeleton in the Campaign folder. A source World the DM names supplies material only.
- **Fork.** A parallel group in a World whose Campaign is still active. Running two groups at once forks the World (ADR 0004), so this is a new setting with the active World as its source.
- **Existing.** A successor once the World's earlier Campaign ends. The World overview and setting pages stay where they are and this Campaign links them. Step 3 lifts the ones it plays into the shared folders.

## Steps

1. **Ground.** Read `user-config.md` and `docs/wiki-layout.md`, then ADRs `docs/adr/0003-ask-about-intent-never-bookkeeping.md`, `docs/adr/0004-campaigns-move-the-world-forward.md`, `docs/adr/0009-pcs-pulled-from-public-dndbeyond-characters.md` and `docs/adr/0010-content-is-gated-deterministically.md`. Read `wiki/dm-settings.md` for the defaults a Campaign inherits. Resolve the repository root and target Wiki before any Wiki operation. A filesystem target the caller assigns takes precedence over the active Campaign in `user-config.md`.

   When the World already has a Campaign, read that Campaign's `campaign-config.md`, then its `hot.md` (orientation, not evidence), its folder's `index.md` and the last ten entries of its `log.md`. Read a source World the same way, for material only.

   Search the Wiki (`.omp/AGENTS.md` § Wiki access) for the new Campaign's context. Retrieve the World overview and its Calendar, plus the Locations where play starts. Look for the Factions whose agendas will drive Threads and House Rules in force. For a successor, also look for the earlier Campaign's overview, its last Recap, its PCs, and the Threads and Quests whose pressures carry over. Which pages bear on the request is your judgement; read each page behind a hit in full, since a snippet only says where to read.

   A World has one active Campaign at a time, while earlier Campaigns remain records of what changed it. When the World the DM names has an active Campaign, establish the DM's intent: a successor after that Campaign ends (Existing), or a parallel group (Fork). Use an answer already supplied; otherwise ask about that intent during Talk. A new request alone does not establish that the earlier Campaign ended. Preserve the earlier Campaign's folder, PCs, Threads, House Rules, `hot.md` and played Sessions as they stand.

   Use the starting state the DM specifies. For Canon, use what the DM says first, then what the Wiki says. When a successor's DM leaves the starting state open, use the end of the earlier Campaign's last Session with a Recap. When the Wiki records a Session as played that the DM calls unplayed, leave those pages unchanged, start from the last Session the DM counts as played, and record the conflict for your report. **Done when** the target Wiki, the setting (New, Fork or Existing), any source World or earlier Campaign and its state are known, and for a successor, the starting state.

2. **Talk.** Start from the DM's supplied vision and ask only about missing intent, talking with the DM per `docs/agents/co-writing.md`. Use an answer already in the request without asking again. When the request supplies every point below, or leaves the rest to you with "you decide", skip the interview and go to step 3. Cover:
   - for a New or Forked setting: the World's name, premise, feel and inspirations; how magic, technology, Deities and the dead work, and how common they are; the starting Region and the trouble that makes it interesting; the Calendar (months and lengths, weekdays, year numbering and epoch, or permission to propose these); and existing must-haves, such as a named villain, Settlement, war or mystery, and any material they want reused;
   - the Campaign name, premise, central tension, tone, themes and anything the table keeps out of play. Capture tone as one sentence the DM could say aloud about their table, plus its two consequences in the DM's words. The first is how it builds an NPC, as a short formula such as image and competence with one revealing need and one fixation. The second is what it adds to a scene beyond the external threat, such as the small human conflict riding the stakes;
   - the starting Location and in-world date in that World's Calendar;
   - how the Party comes together;
   - every Player and intended PC, their public D&D Beyond character URL, known backstory, goals, bonds, fears and the DM's plans for them. When a PC's story side is thin and the DM requests help, offer optional prompts that draw out the PC pulls `plan-session` strains: two that oppose each other and the want they never cleanly arrive at. Useful prompts ask what the PC is trying to become and what they are afraid of losing. Others ask who they love or loved, what personal failure would look like and what red button makes them throw caution to the wind. Answers are ordinary `Story` and `Goals and bonds` content, and the DM may skip any prompt;
   - cadence, Session length, table agreements and any Campaign House Rules.

   Inherit established World facts, the earlier Campaign's tone and table boundaries when the DM keeps them, and `DM Settings` defaults unless the DM changes them. Treat unspecified PC story facts as unknown. Creative delegation from the DM still leaves a PC's public character URL to the DM, so ask for actual links where needed. When the DM founds the setting before the Players are settled, record the Party as a gap for step 6. Summarise the settled vision as the basis for work, and go on building without waiting for approval of a draft. Choose the Campaign folder, page names, template placement and other bookkeeping yourself from this skill. **Done when** each detail is answered, explicitly delegated, inherited or recorded as a gap or missing prerequisite, the one-active-Campaign intent is settled and the Campaign folder is named. Then proceed directly to writing.

3. **Setting.** Settle where the World's pages come from.
   - **New or Fork.** Follow [`references/new-setting.md`](references/new-setting.md) to plan and build the World skeleton in `<campaign-folder>/`.
   - **Existing.** List each setting page this Campaign will use and mark it linked or lifted. Lift the World overview, and each page this Campaign's own pages put into play: the Location where the Party starts, and each Faction or NPC a new Thread drives or casts. A House Rule this Campaign keeps lifts too. Move each lifted page whole, its filename and content unchanged, from `<earlier-folder>/<Kind>/<slug>.md` to `<Kind>/<slug>.md`, and the World overview from `<earlier-folder>/<world-slug>.md` to `<world-slug>.md`. Images it embeds stay where they are. A page this Campaign only cites stays in its folder, linked from this Campaign's pages, and played Session pages always stay with their Campaign.

   **Done when** the New or Fork skeleton meets the reference's criterion, or every listed page of an Existing setting is linked or lifted and each lifted page sits in its shared folder with its content unchanged.

4. **Overview and config.** Read `wiki/templates/Campaign.md` and `wiki/templates/campaign-config.md`. Write `<campaign-folder>/<campaign-folder>.md` (title: the Campaign's name) and `<campaign-folder>/campaign-config.md` from them. Keep every property and section in template order and remove the `%% %%` guidance. Create a folder only when placing its first page. For each page this skill writes, list in `sources` the repo-relative archived Raw paths (`archive/<file>.md`) its facts come from, or `[]`.

   - **Overview.** In `## At a glance`, write Players using the Player names supplied by the DM's request or the earlier Campaign's overview, paired with their PCs; with the Party a recorded gap, say the Players are still to be set. Add Premise, Party (links to the PC pages), Cadence and Now (where the Party stands at the starting state). The `The Campaign` callout is the spoken opening. Follow `theatre-of-the-mind` for it, using its **Slots** row for World or Campaign pitch. In `## Play`, write bullets for Session length and Table agreements. Add House Rules with a link to each House Rule page in force, an earlier Campaign's rule on its existing page. Then add a bullet linking the config as `[[<campaign-folder>/campaign-config|Tone, themes, Lines and Veils]]`, path-qualified because every Campaign folder has a `campaign-config`. Leave `session_length_hours` blank to inherit `DM Settings`, and set it only for an override. `## Depth`: `### Premise`, `### Themes`, and `### Direction` as step 7 sets out.
   - **Config.** `## Tone` holds the captured tone sentence with its NPC and scene consequences, `## Themes` the gathered themes, and `## Lines and Veils` the table's limits. A config kept from an earlier Campaign is retold in fresh sentences, since the gate's `boilerplate` layer flags a sentence copied verbatim from another page.

   Read each page's own check (`skill://lint` § Commands, **Page check**) and repair every finding. Read the finished config before authoring the remaining Campaign pages. **Done when** both pages satisfy their templates, contain the gathered intent, establish the Campaign's starting situation, and pass their page checks.

5. **Register the config.** In `user-config.md`, under `## Campaign configs`, add a `### <Campaign>` heading after the last entry, with the line `@wiki/<campaign-folder>/campaign-config.md` beneath it. **Done when** that section contains the new heading and import line, and every earlier entry is unchanged.

6. **PCs and sheets.** Read `wiki/templates/PC.md` and `skill://pull-pcs`. Create one page per intended PC in `<campaign-folder>/PCs/`, set `dndbeyond_url` to the public character URL, and link each PC from the overview's Party. Title the page with the PC's name; when an earlier Campaign already has a page of that title, add the Campaign as a parenthetical, `title: <PC name> (<Campaign>)` in `<pc-name>-<campaign-folder>.md`, and link it as `[[<pc-name>-<campaign-folder>|<PC name>]]`. With the Party a recorded gap from step 2, skip the PC pages and the pull, and state the gap in the report.

   The story side is yours. Put the DM's story, including any PC pulls the prompts drew out, in `Story`, `Goals and bonds` and `Plans`, retaining supplied story passages and facts. For a returning PC, write the story side fresh for this Campaign and link the earlier Campaign's PC page for the full Backstory, since the gate flags sentences copied verbatim between pages. Follow `theatre-of-the-mind` for the required `Portrait` callout, using its **Slots** row for Portrait and grounding it in known appearance. Keep the Player-to-PC mapping in the overview because the pull replaces the sheet side.

   The sheet side (`Sheet`, `Spells`, `Inventory`) comes only from D&D Beyond through `bun run cf -- pull`, a live call to the public D&D Beyond service. Run an actual pull, not just a preview:

   ```bash
   bun run cf -- pull --campaign "<Campaign>"
   ```

   The selector is the Campaign's name, as `skill://lint` § Commands defines it. Account for every intended PC from the command's per-PC output and resulting page: `updated` or `unchanged` establishes a fetched sheet; `skipped`, `failed`, a dry run or an unavailable operation does not. Compare `Story`, `Goals and bonds` and `Plans` before and after: the pull leaves all three unchanged. Repair gate findings on the pulled pages without altering the fetched facts. A `boilerplate` finding between two pulled sheet sides of the same character stays as the pull wrote it. Report each one verbatim. Then follow `audit` over the changed PCs and their linked neighbours.

   When D&D Beyond is unreachable, a link is missing, a character is private, or the pull command cannot run, leave that PC's sheet side empty of statistics. State the affected PC, the observed error and the exact action needed to resume. Ask the DM to set a private character to public or supply a missing link. For an unavailable service or command, state that the pull command above must be rerun. Continue the reachable Campaign work and preserve successful pulls. **Done when** every intended PC has a real pull outcome or an explicit prerequisite gap, the story-side comparison is accounted for, or the Party is a recorded gap.

7. **Threads.** First record the DM's commitments that later planning must keep as anchors in the overview's `### Direction`. Write every other attractive future there as a possibility, phrased conditionally. Read `wiki/templates/Thread.md`. Create three to five Threads with `status: active` at `<campaign-folder>/Threads/<thread-slug>.md`, grounded in the retrieved World Factions' current agendas and connected to the premise and known PC goals. A successor's Threads are new pages; the earlier Campaign's Threads stay as its record, and a new Thread links the one it grows from. Each Faction-driven Thread links the Faction behind it and relevant existing cast. It states where the pressure stands and its next development if nobody acts, plus how the Party encounters it, available levers and what resolves it. For a Faction step 3 built, link each Thread it drives under its `### Threads`, with the lever the Thread gives the Party. Keep possible endings conditional: creation establishes pressures and DM anchors, while every outcome that depends on the Players stays a possibility. Read each Thread's own check (`skill://lint` § Commands, **Page check**) and repair every finding. **Done when** `Direction` separates any anchors from possibilities, and every Thread has a source agenda or supplied PC goal, a concrete next development the Party can meet and resolving links.

8. **Starting hot.** Write `<campaign-folder>/hot.md` from `wiki/templates/hot.md`, about 500 words and at most 550. Set `date` to the starting in-world date. Fill `## At a glance` (In-world date, Party at with the linked Location, Active Threads, Last Session, Next), `## Active Threads` (one line per Thread, linked, with its current pressure and next move), `## Last Session` (state that no Session has been played in this Campaign; for a successor, the changed World it inherits, each change linked to its page) and `## Next` (what the first Session can pick up). The Party's experience starts here: inherit the changed World, not the previous Campaign's last Session as this Party's experience. Leave Sessions, Quests and other unused folders uncreated. Later Ingest and Prep update hot.md. **Done when** hot.md agrees with the overview, PCs and Threads and gives `plan-session` a usable starting state.
9. **File.** Run the **CLI contract** below. Generate the index, then close per `skill://lint` § Commands with the Campaign folder, each page step 3 lifted, and every other page this run touched, and resolve each finding the page gate reports without changing the DM's intended facts or the pull-owned sheet sections. After the page gate passes, append one `create` entry titled `New Campaign: <Campaign>`, with a `--page` for every page created, lifted or changed, by vault-relative path. The pull records its own `pull` entry when it changes PCs. Observe the entry written, then run the page gate once more so the finished artifacts, the log included, are covered. **Done when** the root `index.md` lists the Campaign overview and each lifted page, `<campaign-folder>/index.md` lists the Campaign's pages, the creation entry lists the touched pages and the final page gate reports `ok: 0 findings`. Report an unavailable gate or remaining findings as an explicit completion gap.

10. **Report.** Give the DM the pitch, linked Party and initial Threads, each PC's observed pull outcome, and the index/log/gate result. For a New or Forked setting, link the World skeleton's pages and note the important Canon decisions and reused material; for an Existing one, list the pages step 3 lifted. Say what play will fill in later. State any remaining prerequisite precisely, distinguishing it from completed page creation, and any Canon conflict from step 1. When the new Campaign replaces the active one, tell the DM to set **Active Campaign** in `user-config.md`. Offer `/skill:collab-with-me` as the next step. Call the Campaign ready only once every required pull and gate is proved. **Done when** the report matches the saved pages and observed operations.

## CLI contract

Run from the repository root, binding every command to the same repository root, Wiki and template directory (**Another root** in `skill://lint` § Commands states the flags). Generate the index, close per `skill://lint` § Commands over the Campaign folder and every touched page, and append one `create` entry titled `New Campaign: <Campaign>`, repeating `--page` for every touched page by vault-relative path. The pull for an explicit target runs as:

```bash
bun run cf -- pull --campaign "<Campaign>" --vault "<wiki>" --root "<root>" --templates "<templates>"
```

Command output, rather than an intended invocation, establishes execution.
