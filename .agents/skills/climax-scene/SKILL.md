# Climax Scene

The Climax settles the Session's central question under its greatest pressure. Earlier Scenes supply the Party's leverage and the opposition's threat; play determines the result. A fight, exposure, bargain or rescue can carry that weight.

Read and follow `docs/agents/scene-pages.md` for every Climax: it owns grounding, cast, template and placement, Narration, conditional handoffs and the filing gate. Apply the steps below within that procedure; consult its **Encounters** section whenever violence is a playable branch.

## Steps

1. **Trace the payoff.** Read the available earlier Scenes and their conditional outcomes, not just the Scene immediately before this one. In `## Threads`, link each Thread converging here, state the question this confrontation decides for it, and identify its earned lever: a Clue, ally, Item, weakness or earlier choice. In `## Play`, give that lever a concrete effect and rule what happens if the Party arrives without it. Carry earlier losses, spent resources and opposition preparation into the relevant entry branches. Resolve an unplanted dependency through the enclosing Prep, or use established material that serves the same purpose; promised earlier success remains conditional. For a standalone brief without earlier Scene pages, ground leverage in the supplied entry and Canon, and return any proposed earlier setup as a planning requirement rather than a played event. **Done when** every converging Thread has a traceable setup and usable payoff, and each plausible entry state can reach the confrontation.
2. **Choose the confrontation.** Use the chart's card when the incoming fiction supports it; otherwise choose from the catalogue below and reconcile the row with the enclosing Prep. Follow the shared **Scripting the Game** section for chart order and table-time allocation; that method governs where Lazy DM differs. Prefer a Development before an action Climax and a Cliffhanger before a cerebral one. If earlier play can settle the central question, that result hands directly to the Resolution rather than staging a second showdown. **Done when** one card fits the possible arrival states, the stakes exceed the earlier Scenes', and the page has a concrete compression option that preserves the decisive choice when time runs short.
3. **Make the brink playable.** Put the decisive question and what can be saved or lost at a glance. The Opening shows the opposition mid-act, the Location's usable features and the imminent escalation, stopping before its result. Under `## Play`, specify starting positions, access to the objective, the opposition's first move, its response to interference, its desperate move and its exit or surrender terms. Give pressure a trigger, visible warning, consequence and means of interruption; when play stalls, identify the next move that changes the situation. Rule victory and loss beyond hit points, including withdrawal. For each phase used, specify the trigger, perceptible signal, changed situation and new opportunity; use `theatre-of-the-mind` for spoken phase signals and any Revelation. In `## Depth`, give the named Spotlight and the other PCs concrete opportunities to use their distinct goals or abilities without assigning their decisions. **Done when** the DM can run every offered approach, stage feature, roll and escalation from the page and its embeds, with essential information reachable beyond a single successful check.
4. **Balance the opposition.** For every Encounter, including violence after failed talks or exposure, follow the shared **Encounters** procedure. Default to High difficulty unless the chart or DM specifies otherwise. Budget for the PCs who can act in this Scene: list each contributing PC and level, exclude anyone unconscious, catatonic or absent, and record the exclusion with its reason. Fetch the 2024 *XP Budget per Character* table and name it as the Balance's source; guidance offering Easy/Medium/Hard/Deadly thresholds or a creature-count multiplier is the 2014 table — refetch before writing, and compare plain XP sums with no multiplier, stating the comparison those sums actually show. Put the arithmetic and comparison in `#### Balance` within `### Encounter` under `## Play`. A fought Creature appears there only as its `![[Creature#Statblock]]` embed; statistics retyped into the Scene page are replaced by the embed. Account for phases and reinforcements in the forces actually faced; explain how earned allies, terrain, prior depletion and objective pressure affect the numeric estimate. Use the opposition's strongest established tactics while keeping the Party's earned levers effective. **Done when** every fight branch has a sourced 2024 Party-budget comparison naming its contributors, linked combatants embedded rather than copied, and an objective that can be won or lost through the stated rulings.
5. **Hand over consequences.** Fill the template's `## Outcomes` table — a renamed or re-nested substitute fails the gate — with at least three distinct, reachable outcomes, chosen from the confrontation's real possibilities rather than a stock list. For each, state its triggering choice or contest result, what is saved or lost, costs paid, the opposition's fate and the resulting state of every converging Thread. Give the Resolution its entry state, including the Party's Location, conditions, resources and knowledge that matter there; link its assigned page, or return the intended aftermath for charting when this is a bounded standalone request. If the Party avoids the confrontation, rule what the opposition achieves and what aftermath remains. **Done when** victory, costly or partial success, and an adverse or refused outcome are runnable where the fiction supports them, and the Resolution can begin from each row without assuming a win or treating an unplayed result as Canon.
6. **Cold read and file.** Keep the current Climax template's depth-2 spine exactly — At a glance, Threads, Play, Outcomes, Depth — with kind detail in nested sections the template allows, and the card off the finished page. Copy each `sources` entry from an existing `archive/` or `raw/` path as listed, never a name composed from the fiction. Read the result as the DM: the Session's highest-stakes question is answerable through play; every earlier payoff has an effect; every ending has a Resolution handoff. Treat repairable findings on the authored page, spelling and grammar included, as unfinished authoring and fix them through the shared procedure's repair loop; only unavailable tooling or findings the gate cannot repair are named blockers. **Done when** all these conditions hold and the shared procedure's full gate, logged only on its clean exit, and enclosing-operation responsibilities are satisfied.

## Cards

Final Battle and Final Revelation are Scripting the Game's original Climax cards. The other cards extend the catalogue for confrontations the fiction earns. Select one governing shape; another card's technique may support it without adding a second Climax.

| Card | Build into the Scene | What decides the ending |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Final Battle | The strongest established opposition, its objective, three actionable battlefield features, and triggered shifts as forces or terrain change. Give earlier leverage a tactical use and state what the opposition accomplishes while unopposed. | The Party can secure the objective, win at a cost, lose it or withdraw; defeating the opposition alone need not save what matters. |
| Final Revelation | The complete truth in the DM layer, accessible evidence, a reason the relevant people are present, the opposing explanation and the culprit's last response. Rule both correct and mistaken exposure. | Evidence the Party actually presents determines what is exposed and who acts on it; the consequence follows that result. |
| Sacrifice | What a voluntary price can save, who can pay it, how payment works, and the cost of refusing. Include alternatives that established fiction makes possible. | A Player chooses whether their PC pays; accepting, sharing or refusing the price has a ruled consequence. |
| Desperate Gambit | The objective, obstacles and rulings for plausible approaches, when the opposition notices, and what breaks if a crucial part fails. | The Players choose the plan; successful pieces still matter when another fails, allowing a partial result. |
| Reckoning | Each converging debt or threat, its demand and deadline, triage options and what allies can take on. | Each demand answered or left unanswered changes its own Thread; handling one does not silently settle the others. |
| Siege | What is defended, vulnerable approaches, numbered forces and wave triggers, fallback positions and the price of abandoning them. Relief depends on an established source. | Holding, evacuation, negotiated surrender and overrun have distinct consequences for what the Party protects. |
| Negotiation Under Duress | Each side's interests, leverage, acceptable terms and refusal point; a visible deterioration clock; and an Encounter if failed talks can become violent. | Offered terms and established interests decide agreement; a costly deal, broken talks and violence remain possible. |
| Catastrophe | Its established causes and warnings, the threatened people or assets, rate of destruction, routes to shelter, and ruled ways to prevent, redirect or limit it. | Party priorities and actions determine what survives; loss is conditional rather than announced in advance. |
| Trial | The charge, judge's authority and criteria, procedure, each side's evidence and the consequences of the available verdicts. | Presented evidence and argument determine the verdict under stated rulings; one social roll does not replace the whole case. |
| Betrayal Cascade | The betrayer's established motive and earlier tells, who is caught between sides, each offer and the immediate reversal; balance any resulting Encounter. | The Party can use earlier suspicions and preparation, choose a side, expose the betrayal or escape; the reversal follows motive rather than negating their choices. |
