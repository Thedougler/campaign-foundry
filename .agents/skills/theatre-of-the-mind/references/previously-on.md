# Previously On

The Previously On retells last Session to the Players as the next one starts, like a TV show's "previously on": it brings them back into the scene where they left off and reminds them of the story's important moments. Its page is in the folder of the Session it opens, so `Session <N> - Previously On` retells Session N−1. It is the story the Players would tell a friend who missed it. It gives the best moments, told so the friend wishes they had been there, and it ends where play stopped. Past tense, the Party as "you", each PC named when they act. Only what play made true. Prep that play never used stays out.

- **Job.** Bring the table back into last Session's best moments.
- **Build.** Concrete, table-marked moments from the caller's Session Ledger or named Transcript, each PC's supported moment, and only enough connective action to tell them.
- **End.** Inside the actual moment play stopped.

## Steps

1. **Read the Session Ledger, else the Transcript once.** When the caller gives a Session Ledger, read the Ledger whole. Its MOMENT and SAID entries are the table-marked moments, with lines. Read the Transcript only at those lines ±10 for exact wording, and at the stopping point in the Ledger's `## Ends`. Without a Ledger, read the caller's named Transcript start to finish, in consecutive slices if needed. As you read, collect table-marked moments: laughter, cheering, a gasp, a big roll or a repeated line, paired with the concrete action that set it off. Keep each PC's moments and the actual stopping point in context. A Guest character (`CONTEXT.md`) counts as a PC here. The table calls people by their real names too: in anything you take from the Transcript, write the name `user-config.md`'s **Real names** list gives in place of each real name. Leave a gap in the Transcript as a gap, without a Wiki search. Done when every marked moment has its action and every PC's supported moment is accounted for, from a whole read of the Ledger plus the Transcript lines around each moment you may use and around the stopping point (without a Ledger, from one complete read of the Transcript).
2. **Rank them.** Biggest reaction first. Each PC's own moment is one where that PC is the sentence's only hero. A moment two PCs share counts for neither of them. Keep each moment as the one concrete thing that happened (he waved a torch at the ogre and set his own beard alight), never its category (he fought bravely). Done when the selected moments are ranked and each PC's supported individual moment is represented.
3. **Tell it.** The moments get the words, and everything between them passes in a clause or drops out. Link each beat to the last by cause (so, which meant, until) or by a cut across it (but, just as). A string of standalone sentences is a chronicle. For comedy, give the setup its own sentence, then the turn, told straight. Dice and rules become what they looked like: a critical hit is a shot that went exactly where it was aimed. A line someone actually said at the table may be quoted word for word. Done when each selected moment is concrete, connected and sourced to the Transcript, with exact quoted speech.
4. **End** inside the moment play stopped: the blade mid-reach, the water at the hatch. Done when the final sentence matches that Transcript moment and stops inside it.
5. **Check and file.** Run the skill's **Revise** and answer these beside the final check's shared items, quoting the block. Then run **File**, including its page gate unless tooling was explicitly skipped. Done when every applicable item passes with quoted evidence and the callout is filed without changing its title or other page content.
   - Does every moment appear as its concrete action, with more words than the routine around it?
   - Could each player retell their character's best moment from it?
   - Does each comic moment have its setup before its turn?
   - Is every roll, rule and Spell name turned into what it looked like?
   - Is the last sentence inside the stopped moment, with nothing about stopping, the night or the Session?
   - Is each person and thing called what the Players called it at the table?

The DM may invite the Players to retell the last Session before this block is read. Their retelling shows what the table itself marked, and the crafted block then tells the story in full. The block stays the archive of the table's true moments.
