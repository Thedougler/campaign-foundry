# Narration recipes

One recipe per narration slot. Resolve the slot from the page's **type or kind plus the callout's title**, because the title alone does not always identify it.

- A Scene's `Opening` takes the recipe of its kind (**Hook**, **Development**, **Cliffhanger** or **Climax**). A Resolution's `Opening` takes **Closing image**.
- An NPC or Item page's `First look`, and a Creature or Vehicle page's `First sight`, take that subject's recipe below.
- A Region or Settlement `Arrival` takes the overlook recipe. A Site `Entering` takes the threshold recipe.
- Inside a Scene, a callout titled with a subject's name takes **NPC entering**, **Creature entering** or **Revelation**, by its subject and its job.
- `Transition` takes the Transition recipe.
- A `Previously on` callout, whatever spelling the caller used, follows [previously-on.md](previously-on.md). Its slot is still the Previously On.
- Every other uniquely named title keeps its recipe below.

Each recipe gives the **Job** (what the block must do), the **Build** (what goes in) and the **End** (where it stops). Every spoken recipe is told from where the Party stands, the Party as **you**, in the present tense, unless the recipe sets another tense or voice. The Previously On uses the past tense, the Handout speaks as its document and the Portrait uses the third person.

## Openings and first looks

These branch rules apply together with the skill's shared craft on every Scene `Opening` and every physical first look (`First look`, `First sight`, `Arrival`, `Entering`, `NPC entering`, `Creature entering`). They never bind the Previously On, Handout text or the teller-voice recipes.

- **The senses.** Something is already moving. Ground the situation in two grounded channels available to the actual Party, with one of them beyond sight and doing a second job (warm air deeper in the tunnel means something lives ahead), whenever the sources and these PCs' capabilities offer them. Otherwise use the grounded cues that remain. Never assume eyes, lungs, hair, footsteps, a class's senses or a bodily response every listener shares. Contact such as spray, mud or heat is a source-backed environmental detail that touches the body, never an imposed PC sensation or feeling. Keep a capability or state caveat (blindness, darkvision, a scout above the party) beside the cue it affects.
- **Scale.** "Size against something familiar" uses the body's own scales, such as thumb-length, shoulder-high, knee-deep or a hand of flame. Spend the block's one staged comparison on size only when the size itself matters, and stage the one image the table keeps (its back rides level with the eaves). A than-verdict on size (reaches longer than a rowboat) spends the image and draws the style gate (`ai-tells.MicDrop`).
- **The baseline.** Write the place so it is true from any entrance, in any lighting state and at any occupancy. A detail true for only one entrance, light level or occupant goes in a clearly triggered DM note or a later variant. A fixed Scene renders its actual state instead. The baseline never erases a Canon opponent, and never asserts one present in every possible revisit. In reusable place text, position a feature against other features (between the hearth and the door). Use body-relative directions (ahead, on your left) only once the current viewpoint is established.
- **Compression.** Choose words that imply many others: "a storm-beaten fishing village" brings the nets and gulls with it. Give four to six things the Players could picture or act on, and arrange them around the anchor. Describe them with nouns specific to the anchor. Under Chekhov's rule, keep a detail in the block only when it is a route, a Clue, the anchor itself or something else the Players can act on. Move details the Players could picture but cannot use to the DM side.
- **Order.** Put the interactable or immediate decision first or last, never in the attention trough.
- **First-look delivery.** Read the Narration before revealing a tactical map, and reveal the map or sketch immediately after when fight-relevant layout matters, because some Players do not picture anything from prose at all. Spend the spoken words on the situation, the nonvisual cues and the tactically important facts that a map or art cannot show reliably. Leave obvious artwork detail to the art. A required hazard or route is still spoken aloud when the art shows it. State a relevant position explicitly, such as between the Party and the door, in place of a vague "nearby". Give a required hazard, route or decision its own conspicuous sentence, and embed only background detail the first look does not owe. Neighbour areas get one orienting clause each, except in the overlook recipes (Region and Settlement Arrival), whose job is the view.

## Scene openings

A Scene's `Opening` contains only what the Players need for their first decision. That means immediate danger or any immediately blocking feature, what the decision turns on, the ways out, the people and Creatures who matter, the things most likely to be grabbed, and the anchor. Put an obvious lethal or blocking hazard and the routes in this first look even if the map shows them. What a closer look would show belongs in the Scene's Play section, for the DM to give when asked.

### Hook

- **Job.** Grab the table in the first sentence and get them moving.
- **Build.** Open inside the disturbance, the thing that just changed, with the place folded around it. Then the people or threat in motion, the ways out, someone to talk to, and one sense beyond sight. When play resumes mid-scene, open on the frozen moment and bring back only the anchor, what changed and what they need to act.
- **End.** The demand, the blow about to land, the stranger's question or the thing about to get away.

Shape to match (never its words):

> Half the fish market is running for the water, baskets and all, while the other half shouts at them to stop. At the foot of the quay a longboat drifts in on the tide with no one at the oars, its hull scraped pale along one side. Gulls circle it and never land. A smell drifts ahead of the boat, low tide with something sweeter underneath. The harbourmaster's boy tugs at your sleeve and points. The bow knocks against the bottom step, and the canvas over the thwarts moves.

### Development

- **Job.** Put the new source of information in front of the Party so the Session can turn.
- **Build.** Slow the pace and keep the situation first, opening on who is here or on who was here and what they left. Then a sense beyond sight and the anchor, the person, document, body or sign that holds the information. Give them something to handle and someone to talk to, and let the evidence carry the conclusion.
- **End.** The NPC's first line, the object set before them or the detail that does not fit.

### Cliffhanger

- **Job.** Danger now.
- **Build.** The threat in the first sentence. Then, fast, where the Party stands, the routes and cover, what is closing, collapsing or chasing, and how near it is. Short sentences where the danger lands and one longer one to lay out the space.
- **End.** The threat a heartbeat away, such as the second beam groaning or the pursuers rounding the corner.

### Climax

- **Job.** The whole confrontation at the brink.
- **Build.** The opposition doing the thing the Party came to stop, what is at stake and where it is, the features the fight can use, who stands between the Party and the goal, and the pressure rising. Everything a player needs to plan a first move is here. The Party learned the villain's plan earlier. A taunt is a line the DM spends during the fight.
- **End.** The escalation, the last moment before it cannot be stopped.

### Closing image

- **Job.** Show what the Session changed, and let it land.
- **Build.** Time and place after the Climax, the visible consequence, the cost still showing, and people reacting through what they do. Each PC's reaction belongs to its player.
- **End.** A still, clear image of the settled aftermath. Questions and new threats wait for the next Session.

### Transition

- **Job.** Move the action across an unplayed stretch of time or space and place the Party at the next decision.
- **Build.** State what has changed since the last slot, the route or duration only as much as the table needs, and one sensory or physical cue at the destination. Keep constants and neighbour areas to a clause. Put travel complications, secrets and NPC plans in DM-side notes or a later Scene.
- **End.** The first perceivable cue at the new place, before the next player action.

## Inside a Scene

A Scene skill adds these as extra `[!narration]` callouts under the Scene's Play or Encounter, titled with the subject's name.

### NPC entering

- **Job.** Make this person memorable and approachable here.
- **Build.** Take their feature from their page's First look so the table recognises them, said fresh, plus what this Scene has changed (a wound, wet clothes, a new companion) and what they are doing now. They sit or stand somewhere in the room, placed in the Party's sight from the first sentence.
- **End.** When the meeting is fixed, their attention landing on the Party with their one line (**People** in the skill). Otherwise, what they are doing.

### Creature entering

- **Job.** This Creature, here, now.
- **Build.** Silhouette, how it moves, the dangerous part, its size against something familiar, and what it was already doing before the Party came. Give its kind only when the characters would know it. Several of the same Creature each get one visible trait the table can call them by (the one with the split ear).
- **End.** The next thing it is about to do, before contact.

### Revelation

- **Job.** Land a major reveal so the table feels it.
- **Build.** The familiar first, then the one thing that contradicts it, then the central image. No explanation of what it means.
- **End.** The central image, with nothing after it.

## First meetings on World pages

The Party meeting the subject as it usually is, with no event. A Scene that uses the subject later says it fresh from these facts. Use the selected Build and End below through this construction:

1. **Frame.** Write the first sentence with the subject acting or positioned in the world. In second-person recipes, place it in relation to **you** or **your**, in the present tense. The Portrait keeps its third-person voice. Leave PC perception, action and feeling to the Players. Done when you can quote the subject and its world action or position from the actual opening, plus the listener-facing words where they are required.
2. **Develop.** Before adding speech, build three to five substantive narration sentences. Each contributes a distinct sourced cue, action or spatial relation from the selected Build, with a hand-off to the next. Give those details complete sentences rather than packing them into compound clauses. Develop the same situation, within the recipe's End. Done when you can number three to five sentences in the actual draft and state each one's contribution and source. Quoted speech and a speech-only lead-in contribute zero to this count.
3. **Finish and check.** Where the recipe ends on speech, append the speaker's lead-in and complete line in their own voice, preserving supplied speech word for word, then stop. Otherwise stop at the selected End. After any revision, recount the substantive narration and recheck the opening in the finished block. Done when that block still contains three to five sourced narration sentences and its opening satisfies Frame without prescribing PC behaviour, with any supplied line exact, counted separately and placed last.

- **First look (NPC).** Build it from a first read (the harbourmaster, a broad woman in a coat two sizes too big), the one feature and what they are usually doing. End on their complete first line.

  > Down the quay from you, a broad woman in a harbour coat two sizes too big sits on an upturned crate, opening oysters with a knife worn thin as a leaf. Each empty shell she flicks into the water. She splits the next one loose and holds it out to whoever is nearest. "You're standing in my light."

- **First sight (Creature).** Build it from silhouette, movement, its strangest feature, the part about to be used, its size against something familiar, a sound or smell, what it does at rest, and a visible sign of each signature ability. End with it at rest, before it notices anyone.
- **First look (Item).** Build it from the plain noun, its size against a hand, material, wear and marks, one sense beyond sight, and a visible sign of each hidden property. End on the sign that invites a closer look.
- **First sight (Vehicle).** Build it from its outline and size against its berth, how it rides, how people get aboard, one working detail and who is aboard doing what. End on the way aboard.
- **Arrival (Region, Settlement).** Build it from the approach or overlook as a traveller meets it and the landmark that orients a newcomer, with the place going about its business. End on the landmark or the way in.
- **Entering (Site).** Build it from the threshold at body scale, what is moving, one sense, and the ways onward. End on something they can use.
- **Public face (Faction).** Build it from members at work as the Party first meets them, how they hold themselves, and the name people use for them. End on the name.
- **The offer (Quest).** Build it from the request, rumour or notice as the Party meets it, who offers it and what they want. End on the ask.
- **Portrait (PC).** Build it from how the other characters see this PC, covering face, build, clothing, posture and one detail beyond sight. Third person, present tense, with the PC's name, since this describes one PC to the others. End on the detail a player would use to describe them.

## Voices

- **As it is told (Lore).** The version people in the World say aloud, in a teller's voice and words. It may differ from the truth on the DM's side. End on the closing line tellers always use.
- **Invocation (Deity).** A devotee's words, or the sign that the Deity is present, as a bystander meets it. End on the sign.
- **Casting (Spell).** What a bystander sees, hears and feels as the Spell takes hold, with no caster, target or Party named. End with the effect in place, before any save or hit.
- **Handout text.** Give the Players a document they can reread and use. Build it from the document's own words in its author's voice, preserving supplied payload verbatim and including only source-backed document details. Format it to be read on the page. Its claims are its author's, whether true or false. A requested new page uses the Wiki's `templates/Handout.md`. Fill its provenance from the source pages you were given, and add only the links the request specifies. The punctuation hard line still applies. End when the document has given its payload.
- **The World / The Campaign.** A pitch to the Players about what this World feels like, or about the Party's situation as the Campaign opens, followed by the promise of what they will do. About 100 words. End on the promise.
