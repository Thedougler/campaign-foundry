# Narration recipes

The shared rules every recipe runs through, and the recipes kept in this file. The skill's **Slots** table gives the file and heading containing each slot's recipe, here or in the content skill that writes the page.

Each recipe gives the **Job** (what the block must do), the **Build** (what goes in) and the **End** (where it stops). Every spoken recipe is told from where the Party stands, the Party as **you**, in the present tense, unless the recipe sets another tense or voice. The Previously On uses the past tense, the Handout speaks as its document and the Portrait uses the third person.

## Openings and first looks

These branch rules apply together with the skill's shared craft on every Scene `Opening` and every physical first look (`First look`, `First sight`, `Arrival`, `Entering`, `NPC entering`, `Creature entering`). They never bind the Previously On, Handout text or the teller-voice recipes.

- **The senses.** Something is already moving. Ground the situation in two grounded channels available to the actual Party, with one of them beyond sight and doing a second job (warm air deeper in the tunnel means something lives ahead), whenever the sources and these PCs' capabilities offer them. Otherwise use the grounded cues that remain. Route each cue to the PC who can perceive it, by name: the one with darkvision sees the outline while the one with the keen nose smells oil. Keep a capability or state caveat (blindness, a scout above the party) beside the cue it affects. A spatial moment may open on the body at the threshold, with the temperature before anything is seen. The sensation is the DM's to give and the feeling stays with the player.
- **Scale.** Give size and distance the way the table will use them: feet, paces, a ceiling height, a count (about twelve feet up on a brass rod, a ceiling twenty feet high), or a body scale (thumb-length, shoulder-high, knee-deep). Wonder at scale rides a number, such as three chambers of a vault that has a hundred. Spend the block's one staged comparison on size only when the size itself matters, and stage the one image the table keeps (its back rides level with the eaves). A than-verdict on size (reaches longer than a rowboat) spends the image and draws the style gate (`ai-tells.MicDrop`).
- **The baseline.** Write the place so it is true from any entrance, in any lighting state and at any occupancy. A detail true for only one entrance, light level or occupant goes in a clearly triggered DM note or a later variant. A fixed Scene renders its actual state instead. The baseline never erases a Canon opponent, and never asserts one present in every possible revisit. In reusable place text, position a feature against other features (between the hearth and the door). Use body-relative directions (ahead, on your left) only once the current viewpoint is established.
- **Zoom.** Move from wide to mid and then onto the story object, from the plain to the fort on its hill and then to the black pennants where its crest should fly. Choose words that imply many others: "a storm-beaten fishing village" brings the nets and gulls with it. Name things with specific nouns, worn and social in Brennan's way (firkins, a crust of bread, rusted breastplates) and measured and crafted in Mercer's (a brass rod, a lacquered hatch). Under Chekhov's rule, keep a detail in the block only when it is a route, a Clue, the anchor itself or something else the Players can act on. Move details the Players could picture but cannot use to the DM side.
- **Order.** An immediate hazard may lead. The story object or the decision lands last, never in the attention trough.
- **First-look delivery.** Read the Narration before revealing a tactical map, and reveal the map or sketch immediately after when fight-relevant layout matters, because some Players do not picture anything from prose at all. Spend the spoken words on the situation, the nonvisual cues and the tactically important facts that a map or art cannot show reliably. Leave obvious artwork detail to the art. A required hazard or route is still spoken aloud when the art shows it. State a relevant position explicitly, such as between the Party and the door, in place of a vague "nearby". Give a required hazard, route or decision its own conspicuous sentence, and embed only background detail the first look does not owe. Neighbour areas get one orienting clause each, except in the overlook recipes (Region and Settlement Arrival), whose job is the view.

## Scene openings

A Scene's `Opening` contains only what the Players need for their first decision. That means immediate danger or any immediately blocking feature, what the decision turns on, the ways out, the people and Creatures who matter, the things most likely to be grabbed, and the anchor. Put an obvious lethal or blocking hazard and the routes in this first look even if the map shows them. What a closer look would show belongs in the Scene's Play section, for the DM to give when asked.

### Hook

- **Job.** Grab the table in the first sentence and get them moving.
- **Build.** Open inside the disturbance, the thing that just changed, with the place folded around it. Then the people or threat in motion, the ways out, someone to talk to, and one sense beyond sight. When play resumes mid-scene, open on the frozen moment and bring back only the anchor, what changed and what they need to act.
- **End.** The demand, the blow about to land, the stranger's question or the thing about to get away.

Shape to match (never its words):

> Half the fish market is running for the water, baskets and all, while the other half shouts at them to stop. At the foot of the quay a longboat drifts in on the tide with no one at the oars, its hull scraped pale along one side. Gulls circle it and never land. A smell drifts ahead of the boat, low tide with something sweeter underneath. The harbourmaster's boy tugs at your sleeve and points. The bow knocks against the bottom step, and the canvas over the thwarts moves.

### Development

- **Job.** Put the new source of information in front of the Party so the Session can turn.
- **Build.** Slow the pace and keep the situation first, opening on who is here or on who was here and what they left. Then a sense beyond sight and the anchor, the person, document, body or sign that holds the information. Give them something to handle and someone to talk to, and let the evidence carry the conclusion, each clue with its cause in plain view.
- **End.** The NPC's first line, the object set before them or the detail that does not fit.

### Cliffhanger

- **Job.** Danger now.
- **Build.** The threat in the first sentence. Then, fast, where the Party stands, the routes and cover with their distances, what is closing, collapsing or chasing, and how near it is in feet or strides. One long sentence lays out the space and short ones land the danger.
- **End.** The threat a heartbeat away, such as the second beam groaning or the pursuers rounding the corner.

### Climax

- **Job.** The whole confrontation at the brink.
- **Build.** The opposition doing the thing the Party came to stop, what is at stake and where it is, the features the fight can use with their heights and distances, and who stands between the Party and the goal while the pressure rises. Everything a player needs to plan a first move is here. The Party learned the villain's plan earlier. A taunt is a line the DM spends during the fight.
- **End.** The escalation, the last moment before it cannot be stopped.

### Closing image

- **Job.** Show what the Session changed, and let it land.
- **Build.** Time and place after the Climax, the visible consequence, the cost still showing, and people reacting through what they do. Each PC's reaction belongs to its player.
- **End.** The Session's last image, chosen by what it needs. A decompression coda is a still, quiet image of the settled aftermath, opened out to its scale (the ship under way, the watch fire burning down). A mid-effect cut stops inside a new effect before it resolves, for a Session that ends on a stinger (`docs/agents/narrative-devices.md` § Closing stinger). Questions wait for the next Session.

### Transition

- **Job.** Move the action across an unplayed stretch of time or space and place the Party at the next decision.
- **Build.** State what has changed since the last slot, the distance and time in plain numbers (two days, about forty miles) as far as the table needs them, the land changing under the Party as they go, and one sensory or physical cue at the destination. Keep constants and neighbour areas to a clause. Put travel complications, secrets and NPC plans in DM-side notes or a later Scene.
- **End.** The first perceivable cue at the new place, before the next player action.

## Inside a Scene

A Scene skill adds these as extra `[!narration]` callouts under the Scene's Play or Encounter, titled with the subject's name.

### NPC entering

- **Job.** Make this person memorable and approachable here.
- **Build.** Take their feature from their page's First look so the table recognises them, said fresh, plus what this Scene has changed (a wound, wet clothes, a new companion) and what they are doing now. They sit or stand somewhere in the room, placed in the Party's sight from the first sentence.
- **End.** When the meeting is fixed, their attention landing on the Party with their one line (**People** in the skill). Otherwise, what they are doing.

### Creature entering

- **Job.** This Creature, here, now.
- **Build.** Silhouette, how it moves, the dangerous part, its size in feet or against something familiar, and what it was already doing before the Party came. Layer the reveal from what it lies on toward its body and the sound it makes. Give its kind only when the characters would know it. Several of the same Creature each get one visible trait the table can call them by (the one with the split ear).
- **End.** Its eyes finding the Party, or the next thing it is about to do, before contact.

### Revelation

- **Job.** Land a major reveal so the table feels it.
- **Build.** The familiar first, then the one thing that contradicts it, then the central image. The biggest reveal gets the fewest words, and nothing explains what it means.
- **End.** The central image, with nothing after it.

## First meetings on World pages

The Party meeting the subject as it usually is, with no event. A Scene that uses the subject later says it fresh from these facts. Use the selected Build and End, below or in the file **Slots** gives for the slot, through this construction:

1. **Frame.** Write the first sentence with the subject acting or positioned in the world. In second-person recipes, place it in relation to **you** or **your**, in the present tense. The Portrait keeps its third-person voice. Leave each PC's actions, decisions and emotions to the Players. Done when you can quote the subject and its world action or position from the actual opening, plus the listener-facing words where they are required.
2. **Develop.** Zoom in the way of the slot's exemplar DM. Brennan's way walks a person or a place through what it is doing, its worn nouns and a comparison to somewhere the table knows. Mercer's way walks a space or a body in order, measured, with the reveal layered toward its eyes or its working part. Each sentence gives a distinct sourced cue, action or spatial relation and hands off to the next, and the long breath ends on a short sentence. Done when you can state each sentence's contribution and source, and the zoom stops at the selected End.
3. **Finish and check.** Where the recipe ends on speech, append the speaker's lead-in and complete line in their own voice, preserving supplied speech word for word, then stop. Otherwise stop at the selected End. After any revision, recheck the opening and the landing in the finished block. Done when the opening satisfies Frame, the block stops at its End within the band, and any supplied line is exact and placed last.

- **First sight (Creature).** Build it from silhouette, movement, its strangest feature, the part about to be used, its size in feet or against something familiar, a sound or smell, what it does at rest, and a visible sign of each signature ability. End with it at rest, before it notices anyone.
- **First look (Item).** Build it from the plain noun, its size against a hand, material, wear and marks, one sense beyond sight, and a visible sign of each hidden property. End on the sign that invites a closer look.
- **First sight (Vehicle).** Build it from its outline and size against its berth, how it rides, how people get aboard, one working detail and who is aboard doing what. End on the way aboard.
- **Arrival (Region, Settlement).** Build it from the approach or overlook as a traveller meets it, wide to near, the landmark that orients a newcomer, the place going about its business, and the one thing out of place. End on the landmark, the way in or the thing out of place.
- **Entering (Site).** Build it from the threshold as the body meets it (temperature, footing, light), the measured space beyond, what is moving, one sense, and the ways onward with their distances. End on something they can use.
- **Public face (Faction).** Build it from members at work as the Party first meets them, how they hold themselves, and the name people use for them. End on the name.
- **The offer (Quest).** Build it from the request, rumour or notice as the Party meets it, who offers it and what they want. End on the ask.
- **Portrait (PC).** Build it from how the other characters see this PC, covering face, build, clothing, posture and one detail beyond sight. Third person, present tense, with the PC's name, since this describes one PC to the others. End on the detail a player would use to describe them.

## Voices

- **As it is told (Lore).** The version people in the World say aloud, in a teller's voice and words. It may differ from the truth on the DM's side. End on the closing line tellers always use.
- **Invocation (Deity).** A devotee's words, or the sign that the Deity is present, as a bystander meets it. End on the sign.
- **Casting (Spell).** What a bystander sees, hears and feels as the Spell takes hold, with no caster, target or Party named. End with the effect in place, before any save or hit.
- **Handout text.** Give the Players a document they can reread and use. Build it from the document's own words in its author's voice, preserving supplied payload verbatim and including only source-backed document details. Format it to be read on the page. Its claims are its author's, whether true or false. A requested new page uses the Wiki's `templates/Handout.md`. Fill its provenance from the source pages you were given, and add only the links the request specifies. The punctuation hard line still applies. End when the document has given its payload.
- **The World / The Campaign.** A pitch to the Players, in the DM's pitch register (`user-config.md` § Prose voice), about what this World feels like, or about the Party's situation as the Campaign opens, followed by the promise of what they will do. About 100 words. End on the promise.

## Cues

A Cue is a short spoken line on a DM-side line, between `==` marks right after its trigger (`CONTEXT.md` **Cue**). Its slot is the trigger, and the recipes below give the Job and the End in one line each. One to three sentences, 60 words at most (`narration/cue-length`). Everything Narration owes applies to the text between the marks: the skill's hard lines, its **Craft** and the `Narration.*` rules. The trigger, rules terms, DCs and damage numbers stay outside the marks, on the DM's side of the line: `Hit, 15 Slashing damage: ==The second claw rakes across your chest.==` Combat Cues follow Mercer's way and every other Cue follows Brennan's. When a Cue gives an NPC's answer, on any trigger, it is the spoken line itself in that person's diction, their action first and then the line, never a report that they answered.

- **Check result.** Give a Cue for every result the check can produce, a Critical Cue included wherever the check can critically succeed. Each result tier buys exactly that much more fiction, the deeper tier a deeper look. A success shows the next true thing with its cause ("one stone sits smoother than the rest, polished by years of fingertips"). A failure never blanks. It installs a confident false belief the Party can act on, or a consequence with its cause in the same breath. Mercer's constraint works too: "it slips from you", or "this blocks you" where the information is denied. The Critical Cue pays out world-law: "Here's what you would know", then one rule of how this World works.
- **Lore when asked.** Answers the question a player asked, in the voice of the NPC who knows it or as "Here's what you would know". One fact, its source and how far that source can be trusted.
- **NPC parting line or status move.** The NPC's action first, then one line in their diction (`npc-design` [craft.md, Voice](../../npc-design/references/craft.md#voice)), such as a trap question, a courteous threat or a parting line paused in the doorway, or help offered with its limit ("to the edge of our duty, and no further").
- **Player gambit.** Yes-and with an instant consequence. The idea works inside its own boundary, and the World answers in the same breath. A risky idea is taxed in the fiction (noise, time, a witness).
- **Combat hit, miss or enemy turn.** A hit runs cause, then image, with the number outside the marks. For a miss, show what turned the blow (sparks skating off the rim of a shield). On an enemy turn, speak its reasoning in plain view: "It doesn't know where you are, so it goes for the closest body it can smell."
- **Kill handoff.** When a blow can drop a named foe, the Cue lands the blow's image and hands the kill to its player: "How do you want to do this?" The DM finishes the player's framing in the foe's body and closes on a short period.
- **Round repaint.** The top of a round gives the whole changed board in two sentences, the light, the positions and what is now burning, and ends on whoever is up.
