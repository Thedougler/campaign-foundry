# 2024 Influence

## The rule

Retold from the [SRD 5.2.1](https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/srd/5.2/SRD_CC_v5.2.1.pdf): "Social Interaction" in Playing the Game, and the Rules Glossary entries Attitude, Friendly, Indifferent, Hostile and Influence [Action]. The SRD says "monster" for any creature the GM runs, NPCs included. Cite this source when page prose names the rule.

- **Attitude** is how the NPC regards a character. A Friendly NPC is well disposed and inclined to help. An Indifferent one, the default, has no wish to help or hinder. A Hostile one is ill disposed and inclined to hinder. Roleplaying moves Attitude, especially play on the NPC's wants, fears and sympathies. An Influence check decides one request.
- **Influence** is the action a character takes to urge the NPC to do one thing. The player says how the character goes about it, and the GM judges the request against the NPC's desires:
  - **Willing**: the request suits the NPC's own aims. It complies without a roll, in the manner it prefers.
  - **Unwilling**: the request is repugnant to it or runs against its alignment. It refuses without a roll, and no check can change that.
  - **Hesitant**: anything between. Only this case calls for a check.
- **The check.** The GM picks it from the approach. Deceiving or persuading an NPC that understands the speaker takes Charisma (Deception) or Charisma (Persuasion). Intimidation and amusement take Charisma (Intimidation) and Charisma (Performance). Coaxing a Beast or Monstrosity takes Wisdom (Animal Handling). The DC defaults to `max(15, the NPC's Intelligence score)`. A Friendly NPC grants Advantage on the check, and a Hostile one imposes Disadvantage.
- **Results.** On a success, the NPC does what it was urged to do. On a failure, that same urging cannot be tried on it again for 24 hours, or a span the GM sets.

Any deviation from this rule is a declared invention, never described as official Influence.

## Procedure

1. **Frame the request.** State what the NPC is after now, what the Party asks, and the cost or threat to the NPC.
2. **Separate states.** Record Attitude and request willingness separately, each with the reason in the NPC's goals, fears and limits.
3. **Resolve willingness.** A request aligned with the NPC's goals or values is Willing. One that crosses an explicit limit, oath or core value is Unwilling. Hesitant covers the requests left between.
4. **Set the check.** For each Hesitant request, write the roll as `Ability (Skill)` from the approach that fits this NPC's want, fear, limit and contradiction, with its DC and the Attitude's Advantage or Disadvantage. Evidence, a favour, a gift or a credible threat are framings of one of the rule's approaches, and each still goes through the check.
5. **Resolve movement.** Roll only when the outcome is uncertain and a miss changes the situation. State success and miss separately. Success gets the urged act. A miss closes that urging for the rule's wait and spends time, trust, leverage, safety or opportunity in the fiction. Beliefs and choices stay the NPC's own after either result.
6. **State the new position.** Say what the NPC will now do and what they refuse. Add what they need next, with its cost and the next interruption point.
