# Evidence the Party can use

Read this when placing a hidden truth, hazard, Item, inhabitant or trace of history. Work from the Canon inventory and selected sources; put the resulting facts into the owning feature, route, service or area rather than publishing a process table.

## Place and reveal

Locate each important element and explain why it is there. Give it a concrete tell: a sound with a source, a visible alteration, a pattern of use or a witness's behaviour. The DM's adjacent entry states the exact truth, who caused it, what is at stake and what an investigation or interaction reveals. A vague implication is not a DM answer.

Narration carries only what is perceivable from its viewpoint. Show an important tell where the Party would actually meet it, not necessarily in the entrance callout. Reuse the same feature name on both sides of the page so a question about the detail finds its answer immediately.

For a hidden truth needed to make an important choice, provide three independent Clues in different reachable features, areas or interactions. Vary how the truth can be learned: a physical trace, a person or record, an observable operation or consequence. State how each can be discovered. Direct inspection of an obvious fact reveals it; a check resolves genuine uncertainty and charges a stated cost on failure without making the only required revelation unreachable.

## Hazards

Give the DM all six answers for each hazard:

- **Sign:** what warns the Party before commitment.
- **Trigger:** the action, event or threshold that activates it.
- **Effect:** affected space or targets, duration, movement, damage, condition or other consequence, with sourced rules where needed.
- **Counterplay:** what preparation, tools, timing, abilities or intervention can do.
- **Bypass:** an alternate route or method and its cost.
- **Leverage:** a discoverable fact, resource or relationship that improves the Party's options.

State any check/save and what follows on success or failure. Honour actual capabilities such as flight or social authority; a limit needs a reason in the fiction and a way to learn it. A fixed environmental hazard belongs to its Site; a portable hazard uses an Item owner. Regions and Settlements explain exposure on their routes and link the detailed owner when one exists.

## Other evidence

For an Item, state its position, owner, why it is here and how it can be reached; link its full rules. For an inhabitant, state what it is doing and wanting now, or the trace it left if absent. For history, place surviving evidence and its explicit explanation beside the relevant structure. For a Faction, show how its local claim affects access, trade or behaviour and link its owner.

**Complete when** every relevant inventory fact is retained in an identifiable place or excluded from local play with a reason, every consequential hidden truth has independent discoverable Clues, and each hazard has a complete, answerable procedure.
