## At a glance

- **Character.** A band of dead calm water running below the [[Midchain]]'s southern edge. The water goes mirror-still, sails hang slack, and sound crosses it too clearly.
- **Held by.** Ungoverned. Midchain pilots sell drift intelligence, opportunistic pirates work the becalmed hulls, and a black dragon patrols the deeper southern channels.
- **Changing.** The band drifts 50 to 100 miles north or south with the season. Captains who track its position keep to the Midchain back channels instead, and ships that do not sit and wait.
- **Crossing.** Round it through the Midchain back channels for a pilot's fee, or cross by tow, days at the boats with water and crew spent.
- **Danger.** Water depletion, jellyfish blooms that foul a hull, wasps and crocodiles in the mangrove shallows, oared raiders at dusk, and the dragon below.

Winds near the band turn more westerly, helping westbound ships use the [[Midchain]]'s back channels to avoid Strait inspection.

> [!narration] Arrival
> The wind stops between one watch and the next, leaving your sails slack against the masts and the water flat from rail to horizon. On that flat water the ship's own sounds return to you louder than they left, and from the bow the lookout's call comes down whole. Pale rafts of jellyfish drift with the still water, packed so close the hull will drag going through. High overhead frigate birds wheel in numbers. They go nowhere. Your charts show open water here, yet the frigate birds knew before you did.

## Play

### Travel

The boundary is not marked on most charts. One watch a ship runs in trade winds, the next the wind is gone, and the calm closes over the hull. Frigate birds circling overhead in numbers are the standard warning. Jellyfish blooms collecting in the still water come next, and a windless watch means the calm is already closing.

- **Reading the approach.** Wisdom (Survival) DC 12 for the lookout to read the warning signs early. Success turns the ship for the back channels while wind remains. Failure means the calm closes over the ship becalmed.
- **Fixing the edge from inside.** Intelligence (Navigator's tools) DC 10 to work out which way the band's edge lies and hold the shortest tow-line to it. Success saves a day of towing. Failure pushes the tow along the band for an extra day, and every extra day burns water.

The band gives a becalmed ship two ways out, and the choice between them is real:

- **Round by the Midchain back channels.** From the band's northern edge to its far side through the reef harbours of the [[Midchain]], under pilot guidance. It adds days to the crossing and costs a pilot's fee or a bought drift position, and it buys the only outcome with no hull in the still water.
- **Tow across.** From wherever the calm catches the ship to the band's far edge. Ship's boats tow, a mile or two an hour while the crews keep pulling, and the crossing takes days. Supply is water: a tow that outlasts the casks ends in dehydration, and jettisoning weight saves days at the price of everything thrown over. Warning: tow crews in the water at dusk are exactly what the raiders and the dragon's channels are for. Timing helps instead of a third road. The band drifts 50 to 100 miles by season, and a captain who knows where the edge sits this month crosses at the seasonal near side and cuts days off the tow.

Provision at a Midchain harbour before committing to any tow. Every cask the tow drinks comes off that harbour, and the water inside the band is what you carry. Rest aboard while becalmed is safe from weather and exposed to boarders. The flat water carries a whispered order from the mast to the helm, and a night watch keeps quiet. A tow crew rotates in shifts, because towing exhausts, and exhausted crews in the shallows meet what lives there.

### Places

No charted place sits inside the band. The routes that matter lead out of it: the [[Midchain]] back channels to the north, and the long water toward the [[Drowned Maw]] past the Galewall.

### Rumors

- "The calm walks with the seasons. That chart was true the day it was drawn." A Midchain pilot says it while naming a price. True: the band drifts 50 to 100 miles north or south by season. Investigate: buy the drift position, or compare two charts from different years at a Midchain harbour.
- "It circles you once and lets you be. It is the letting-be you should fear." Pilots who run the southern channels say it, and will not say more. True: the dragon uses the flat calm as hunting cover and does not always attack. Investigate: ask a pilot what made the deeper southern channels their own hazard category, and buy the answer.
- "Row when sail can't. A low hull at dusk crosses flat water like a knife." Smugglers say it about the raiders. True: oared cutters move where a becalmed ship cannot choose distance. Investigate: compare a becalmed ship's last heading with where the raiders were next seen.

## Depth

### History

The boundary has never been charted, and the water is ungoverned. What practice exists belongs to the Midchain pilots, who learned to track the band's seasonal drift and turned that knowledge into trade.

### Hidden truths

- **Drift intelligence is money.** Knowing where the band sits and when it will move costs a merchant captain or a smuggler real coin. The pilot's price gives the trade away. A drift position costs more than a course, and the pilots sell to whichever side pays.
- **The channels are a territory.** A black dragon patrols the mangrove channels south of the Midchain's edge. It has used the still water and trapped quiet as hunting cover for so long that Midchain pilots treat the deeper southern channels as a distinct category of hazard. You know the territory by the wake under the keel and by birds that stop landing on the water.
- **The calm compounds the Maw crossing.** A crew bound past the Galewall for the [[Drowned Maw]] loses days it cannot spare to the still water. That loss is its own problem on top of the calm.

### Threads

So far the band lies outside all five active Threads. They keep to Aruhe, Calveno and the hunt routes. The [[Drowned Maw]] crossing is where a becalmed crew hurts most, because the days the calm takes there are days the Maw does not wait for.
