## Areas

Salvagers and investigators can inspect ships that arrived undamaged after their reported loss somewhere else.

- **Wreck shore.** ==Whole hulls sit above the tideline, with sails still rigged and salt crusting their rails.== The ships wash ashore months after sinking in distant waters. Their crews are absent. Inspect names, cargo and surviving records to compare a hull's condition with the account of its loss.
- **Channels between hulls.** ==Water fills the gaps below the grounded ships, while gulls nest in the older rigging.== The tide changes access between wrecks. Low scrub leaves the shore visible, and edible coastal greens can extend stores during a search. A boat avoids dependence on a wading route that the rising tide will cover.

## Hidden truths

Neither the route by which the ships arrive nor the fate of their crews is established. Their intact state and delayed arrival are evidence of the displacement, not proof of a connection to another island's mystery.
