## Areas

A crew chooses its route at the cut, through the reef or round it in open water, and the payoff is time against information. The reef route is slow, hidden and pilot-dependent. Open water is direct, but it is exposed to weather and shows the ship's approach. Which island the cut lies off, which channel it joins and which fleet route it serves are not established.

At the cut: ==The water goes pale and shallow, and broken reef hides the channel from you in both directions. Birds wheel over one stretch and white foam breaks over another. The line of reef markers has gaps in it.==

Reef surface and channel depth change a vessel's speed, what its crew can see and how it escapes. A crew that keeps off the [[midchain-council-dock|Midchain Council Dock]] by pilot or contact faces reef travel like this.

## Hazards

### Shallows

The sign is birds over a shoal, breaking foam and missing reef markers. Any vessel entering the cut meets them, and the reef counts as Difficult Terrain for vessels. A failed passage costs time or position, never the only clue to the route ahead. Reading the signs uses Study.

The crew can hire a pilot or anchor and observe. It can also reroute through open water at the price of exposure.

- Study, success: ==The birds keep to the same stretch and the foam breaks on the stretch beside it, and between them the water stays smooth and dark.==
- Study, failure: ==The foam looked thin where you steered, and the hull slides onto pale shallows. The current drags you sideways along the reef before you can haul round.==

## Likely actions

- **Pilot the cut.** A pilot takes the hull through the reef. The pilot's fee or the cost of bought waypoints is not established.
- **Anchor and observe.** Waiting at anchor costs time. The crew can watch the birds and the foam, and the other boats choosing different channels round the same shoal.
- **Reroute.** The ship stays in open water, which is direct and costs exposure to weather and watchers.
