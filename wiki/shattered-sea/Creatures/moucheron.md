## Tactics

A Moucheron practises the Way of the Five Blades. Each hand wields a sword, and its stinger supplies the fifth blade. It attacks repeatedly and looks for a wounded opponent to feed from. Before a Stinger attack: ==It spreads its swords apart, and the needle beneath them points at your wound.==

Fast flight makes the Moucheron highly mobile. It fights cunningly, evades retaliation and feeds on the enemies it wounds. A Stinger hit lets it leave melee without exposing itself. When it misses a feed, its next attacks become fiercer. Keep beyond its stinger's reach to deny the feeding burst, or force it to spend movement closing rather than circling a wounded ally. Its resistance to magic helps it withstand spells.

Contracted fighters stay with their clients under fire while fed. Completing the hired task ends their part in a fight, but unpaid hunger can turn them against allies quickly. The [[five-blades|Five Blades]] fight as a squad of four and will not split.

## Outside a fight

Fed Moucherons enjoy company and actively seek friendships with giants, but the eagerness for their next blood fix never leaves. An addict's manner runs through their culture: professionals sip from flasks throughout the day, while feral Moucherons go wild in pursuit of a feed. Hunger makes them irritable and withdrawn. They stand roughly pigeon-to-chicken height. [[ket|Ket]] is an ordinary-sized Moucheron. Their bodies resemble little gnomes with four arms and wings.

Mercenaries leaving [[murrat|Murrat]] hire out of [[kalowe|Kalowe]]. Payment is blood rather than gold, usually two to three litres at nightfall. They state that term before signing. Beast blood and blood from creatures of other planes are acceptable. A bloodless assignment still owes the agreed feed. Steady employment gives them patience without making them tame.

Each carries two or three sealed clay keeps. Their stinger's anticoagulant preserves the blood inside for two to three days. Mercenaries decant it into flasks and take frequent sips between feeds. They follow the contract's wording while fed. Experienced captains stock chilled reserves sufficient to feed their hired Moucherons for twice the voyage, so a missed battle does not cost the crew their lives.

### Hunger

Count time since a full feed. A Stinger hit or a willing draw from a Medium or larger creature resets the clock. Their unusually fast metabolism demands frequent drinking, and smaller sips sustain them between full feeds. They divide their daily ration through the day. Hunger resembles a human's need for food on a faster clock: it starts within hours and becomes desperate within a day. These states guide behaviour, separate from the combat traits that track the previous turn.

| Time without a full feed | State | Behaviour |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Just fed | Satiated | Sociable and professional, already anticipating the next sip. |
| Hours | Hungry | Irritable and fixated on blood. Asks for a draw or takes another sip. Allies are in danger if feeding fails. |
| Within a day | Desperate | Takes the easiest blood available, with agreements yielding to survival. |
| Following days | Starving | Hunger worsens as it fights to feed and wastes away. |
| Seven days at most | Dead | A Moucheron cannot survive longer without blood. |

They must consume blood regularly to survive. With enemies to bleed, battle supplies food; without enemies, a hungry Moucheron will kill an ally to feed.

## Ecology

Billions of Moucherons live among Murrat's cliff hollows and canopy, feeding on the island's wildlife. The young begin on animal blood. Kin-groups maintain their own blood economies. Willing bloodletting settles debts and pays for skilled work. It also seals agreements. Between Moucherons, taking blood without consent violates the custom. An outsider approaching the island cannot claim its protection.

Murrat's inhabitants are sentient and nearly feral towards non-Moucherons. Neither law nor government restrains them, and safe passage is impossible. A swarm can drain a human dry in seconds. The [[midchain|Midchain]] considers shipwrecked crews lost and won't send a rescue.

The Five Blades are commonly counted among the Sea's greatest fighting forces. People extend that reputation into a hypothetical. A Moucheron army would be almost unbeatable, but nobody could realistically raise one. The swarm is too vast and disorganised to drill. Feral bloodthirstiness compounds the problem, and an army could not supply enough blood on days without a battle.

## Hidden truths

The [[dravosi-crown|Dravosi Crown]] lists Moucherons alongside the [[rattkin|Rattkin]] under species bounties, but records their killing separately as hazardous wildlife removal at a higher rate. [[barnaby-rook|Barnaby Rook]] took Ket as a specimen because he wanted to breed Moucherons for the Five Blades' strength and fervour. Ket can explain that purpose. The Crown's bounty records show the different classification.
