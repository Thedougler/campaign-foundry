> [!narration] First sight
> You hear a thin squeak first, reedy as a cheap pipe. Then the smell finds you. Sour. Eye-watering. Ahead in the moss sits what looks like a tree stump, until it takes one slow step. It's two legs, each about the length of your own, made of living moss and ancient wood, planted in the mud with nothing above them but air. Where the legs meet, a brass trumpet sits wedged between the cheeks, and yes, those cheeks, its bell hanging low. It settles back into the mud. The bell trembles with another toot.

## Tactics

The glutehorn is a skirmisher whose whole purpose is its audience. On its first turn it opens at maximum range. It uses Slip Away each turn to Disengage and keep moving. If it moves without Disengaging, Opportunity Attacks against it have Disadvantage. Walkin' Toots can poison anyone who starts a turn within 30 feet and can hear or smell it, also imposing Disadvantage on their next save to maintain Concentration. It Kicks only what is already adjacent.

Rectal Reveille fires the moment it is cornered. Before it: ==The squeaking stops dead. The legs plant, and the bell swings round to face you.== Trumpet Blast-Off fires the instant anything makes a melee hit on it or grapples it. It flies 30 feet off, and the gas is left where the Party was standing.

- **Fight at range.** Blast-Off answers only a melee hit or a grapple, so arrows and spells never launch it or seed the gas. It stays in the open and keeps Disengaging, which gives someone the job of cutting off its path.
- **Stay out of the cone.** Reveille is a 15-foot Cone and recharges on a 5 or 6. A Party spread wider than the cone takes it on one or two people, and the round after it fires the glutehorn usually has no cone left.
- **Step around the gas.** The cube lasts until the start of the glutehorn's next turn. Go round its edges to avoid the saving throw while pursuing the creature.
- **Cut the senses.** Walkin' Toots cannot affect someone who can neither hear nor smell the glutehorn, though it keeps tooting.

At 0 hit points: ==It releases one long, mournful note.==

## Outside a fight

A glutehorn strides out with confidence and is delighted to find an audience. It has no drive but performance. It seeks any assembly as an audience for its trumpet-powered concerts. It flees touch, yet stays close enough to be heard and smelled. Each exhale is an unpredictable squeaky toot with a gust of stench that can poison anyone too close. It breathes, speaks and somehow perceives through the trumpet, and it feels the ground around it.

Attempts to silence one are taken as applause. A still glutehorn is easy to take for a mossy tree stump.

The trumpet is part of its body: its means of breathing and speaking, not a detachable instrument. In tense situations its toots come involuntarily.

It is drawn to whatever moment matters most, whether that is a patrol being avoided or a tense negotiation, a eulogy or a proposal. The more serious the moment, the more it wants to be there. It turns up at solemn ceremonies and grand gatherings, and few can stomach the chase. A glutehorn solves nothing and serves nobody's plans. It is an annoyance, a taste of how much the Feywild enjoys messing with mortals.

## Ecology

There are many glutehorns. They live in the Feywild and cross into the mortal world, and on [[huahei|Huahei]] they come through the [[huahei-crossing|crossing]] and turn up in the marsh. Sailors who know Huahei count a glutehorn as the least troublesome fey on the island. They take its squeak and stink as a sign that things are fine. The signs before contact are the air and the ground. The squeak comes first and the smell follows, and a glutehorn standing still looks like a stump of moss and old wood.

On Huahei the story goes that a trickster archfey, tired of the long-winded nobles cluttering his court and their endless speeches, made glutehorns on purpose as the perfect critic. Nobody on Huahei has met him. The punishment was as proportionate and humiliating as you would expect. Their premiere at a grand summer banquet disrupted it so completely that he lost control of them at once. They escaped into the castle walls and tooted softly behind tapestries at the worst moments. Then they found secret ways out into both the fey and mortal realms. He has never publicly acknowledged this. He smiles when asked and changes the subject. A saying attributed to this trickster archfey, who smiles too much, has him wishing only to discourage long speeches and refusing responsibility for what followed.

[[clydes-bestiary-of-oceanic-creatures|Clyde's Bestiary of Oceanic Creatures]] covers the glutehorn in the fourth chapter of its second volume, written by [[algernon-reginald-clyde|Algernon Reginald Clyde]].

When a [[huahei-crossing|Wild Hunt]] is about to come through Huahei, every glutehorn on the island stops tooting. Anyone who knows the island runs for the boats at that quiet.
