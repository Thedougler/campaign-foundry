## Roleplaying

- **Wants.** To finish his current reading from Volume II and return to his field notes.
- **Torn between.** His contempt for every creature he documents and the accuracy that keeps his book useful. When lives are at stake he gives practical advice, and then he resents having given it.
- **Voice.** Precise naturalist terminology. He corrects names and measurements as he goes, and he treats his contempt as an honest scientific response. It fuels his rigour and never replaces it.
- **Opening move.** At a dock, this four-foot gnome argues about someone's pet. He wears good boots and is promoting Volume II of [[clydes-bestiary-of-oceanic-creatures|his Bestiary]], though he insists he is not on tour. His starting Attitude is irritable.
- **Leverage.** His Bestiary is the book sailors use to name what is trying to kill them. He cannot stop correcting an inaccurate creature claim, which hands anyone a way to keep him talking.
- **Opens them up.** Observed behaviour at a named location, backed by a witness's detailed account.
- **Shuts them down.** Anyone who sentimentalises an animal. He hates frogs most of all.
- **Will share.** Whatever he has documented. He wrote the fourth chapter of Volume II on the [[glutehorn|Glutehorn]].
- **If ignored.** He finishes the reading and packs his notes, correcting inaccurate remarks from the audience as he goes.

## History

A publisher commissioned the first volume of the Bestiary when he was in his early thirties, for three months of coastal notes at standard rates. More volumes were demanded, and by the second he understood that he was building a record and not only a reference. His prose treats an evolutionary feature as a flaw, and it counts the ocean's cold indifference to its creatures as the only good thing about them.
