## At a glance

- **Role.** Calveno wreck survivor living alone inland, a former ship's carpenter who refuses to cut, and the strictest living reader of the law.
- **Wants.** One dawn owing nothing. Until then, every open cut sealed before dusk.
- **Voice.** Quiet and exact, apologetic as a man reading out a bill, in a debtor's grammar of cut, claim, owed, marked, paid and clear.
- **Found at.** The [[Old Gardens]] terraces from first light, sealing broken stems along the edges. He sleeps on dead ground, burnt stumps and stone shelves, because as he puts it, "there is nothing left there to owe."

> [!narration] First look
> A wiry man in lashed-together clothes wets river mud and thumbs it into a gash in a terrace vine, counting under his breath. Cord hangs from his belt in a braid of knots, one knot loosened at each dawn. Without looking up he says, "Don't pick up anything you broke. Ask me after, and I'll tell you what you've done."

## Play

- **Opens them up.** Hand him shed fibre or fallen fruit and he talks freely. Ask him about the rule and he walks you through it as arithmetic, tallies and marks with dawn as the settling hour.
- **Shuts them down.** A drawn blade near living wood, or the words "just one branch". He stops talking, backs toward dead ground, and after dusk he will not share a camp with anyone marked.
- **Will share.** The practical law as the inland survivors keep it, fallen fruit, deadwood, loose stone, shed fibre and shell safe to receive. The marks that bring the island's creatures stay on the taker until the next dawn: living growth, dug ground or island flesh. He reads the marking in leaning plants and one silent breath.
- **Will not share.** Anything about the woman in the woods or the voice. The garden's accounts are its own, he says, and the gardener is not his to guess. What he cut in the first days after the wreck.
- **If pressed.** He gives away what he carries and walks for dead ground. Cornered on his past, his hands shake, and he holds up the knotted cord and says, "Notches are cuts. I count like this now."

## Depth

### History

Oren came off the wreck with the Calveno camp, a ship's carpenter taken in the same raid as the rest and wrecked with them on Aruhe's shore. In the first days on the beach he cut green poles for shelters, because the sea had soaked every driftwood log. He speaks of what came at dusk for the pole-cutters, of packing river mud into their wounds until dawn, and he counts "the ones who were not". He left the beach camp and walked inland alone. He seals every cut he passes now and counts on a knotted cord at his belt, because notches are cuts.

### Hidden truths

- He was the best cutter on that beach, and the adze-marks on the abandoned beach shelters are his. A carpenter reads the tool-marks at a glance, and Matteo Scola or the Pantry survivors can name who cut the first poles. The master's hands show in every lashed joint of a man who refuses blades now.
- Sealed stems mark the inland trail the whole way north, and some survivors whisper that the unseen watcher in the garden seals them. Oren has never corrected anyone, because being feared for it keeps desperate people from cutting. His elbows are muddy by midday, and sealed stems stop appearing wherever he camps.

### Threads

He sits in **Taking on Aruhe** as the law's strictest interpreter, and his sealed stems mark safe water and received-fruit caches along the inland trail. Ship versus garden: Matteo calls men like Oren mad. Oren calls men like Matteo running tabs. He is garden-side out of debt alone.
