## At a glance

- **The truth.** The minotaur is a broad, horned humanoid with perfect recall of any passage it has walked, and its culture organizes life around passages, both routes and life stages.
- **Who knows it.** Every port crew that has paid pilot rates knows it. The convention that a pilot commands the helm in narrow water applies whatever flag flies.
- **Limits.** Communities sit wherever the water is hard, and the Wiki does not record a homeland or nation for them.
- **Reaches play through.** Hiring a pilot in the [[Midchain]], the winding interiors of minotaur quarters, and the lost minotaur captain [[Sienne Orre]], whose passage-sense surfaces in [[Delmar Fisk]].

> [!narration] As it is told
> Hire the pilot with the horns and do what the pilot says, or swim. Argue with a minotaur in a channel and you are steering for the bottom. The old sailors say the raging bull is a story for folk who never worked a deck, because a working pilot is calm as a surgeon and twice as expensive.

## Play

- **Players notice.** Minotaur quarters have hallways that turn, with switchback corridors and stairs that double back. Anger builds visibly. The breathing changes and the horns come forward, and a minotaur at that point is extraordinarily dangerous.
- **Clues.** A young minotaur's channel-name, taken from the first passage they ran alone, tells close kin where they were tested. A crew's willingness to sail blind water behind a horned pilot tells you who holds the helm. [[Delmar Fisk]] shows a spatial awareness he has no history to explain.
- **Accounts.** Sailors who have met only the exceptions tell the raging bull and leave it there. Working pilots tell it as the focused calm of a surgeon mid-operation. The folk themselves tell it as passages, where a life is a chart you learn once and never forget.

## Depth

### The full truth

A minotaur is a medium humanoid built heavy, with a bovine head, thick curving horns, broad shoulders and hoofed feet. The horns are a natural weapon dealing 1d6 piercing damage. After moving 20 feet or more straight toward a target, the minotaur can make a bonus-action horn attack, Goring Rush. A failed Strength save leaves the target pushed 10 feet. After a melee hit, it can shove with Hammering Horns, and a failed save leaves the target pushed 10 feet and prone. Labyrinthine Recall is perfect. A minotaur who has walked a passage once can walk it blindfolded twenty years later. The same faculty works on currents, channel marks and reef lines, and it grants advantage on checks to find a way or keep from getting lost.

Passages organize the culture. The word covers a sea route, a life stage and the transition between them. A young minotaur's first solo navigation of a hard channel is the passage-rite, and the channel's name becomes part of how close kin address them. Kinship runs through Soundings. An elder and a calf form a Sounding when the elder shows the calf a passage, and adults form them as equals. A minotaur gathers many small bonds over a life instead of answering one seat of power. Interiors are complex by design. A straight corridor leaves a minotaur exposed the way a windowless room leaves a human. Guests are not expected to find the kitchen. The host guides them, and this is hospitality rather than a test.

At sea the pilot's authority is absolute in narrow water, and even Crown naval officers defer in a reef passage. Temperament runs warm and slow. The horns come forward for concentration as much as for anger. Only the breathing and the stance separate fury from a pilot deep in a chart. Personal names take Greek and Minoan roots, with strong consonants, open vowels and endings in -os, -as and -is. A minotaur in mixed crews often takes a local name, so one in Calveno waters can go by a name indistinguishable from a Tessarine human.

### The captains bound to Delmar

The minotaur the Wiki names at sea is [[Sienne Orre]], who captained the [[Fernen]] in [[Fisk's Fleet]] and ran the fleet's perimeter survey operations. Her passage-sense made her the natural choice. The fleet stole the Pearl of Souls from Umberlee's [[Umberlee's Shrine|shrine]] on [[Vel-Orn]] and sank over the [[Drowned Maw]] when Umberlee struck it. The Party has never been given the captains' names, and the table keeps it that way. Umberlee pressed the five captains' souls into Delmar as punishment and leverage. One of them shows himself when a spell targets him or a green mermaid sign stirs a memory he cannot place.
