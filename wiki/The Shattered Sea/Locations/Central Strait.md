## At a glance

- **Character.** A broad corridor of clear blue-green water, forty to eighty miles across, running between the [[Crown Islands]] and the [[Midchain]]. Deep water, sheltered approaches and a current from astern arrive together almost nowhere else, so nearly everything moving between the colonial homelands and the Midchain uses it.
- **Held by.** The [[Dravosi Crown]] nominally, through the inspection gate at the western mouth and the patrols that stage from [[Harwick]]. In practice the pilots, smugglers, [[Kalowe]] operators and local ports set the terms of any crossing.
- **Changing.** The eastern end deepens toward the [[Drowned Maw]], and the Maw's changing water is already legible in the Narrows as a deeper colour and instruments that stop agreeing.
- **Crossing.** Eastbound runs with wind and current and gains half a knot to a full knot unearned. Westbound takes the whole length against both, tacking between the shores. The inspection at the Tidefall Gate is the toll every hull pays or skirts.
- **Danger.** Reef sharks in the shallows, hunter sharks along the deeper lanes, giant octopuses in the wrecked limestone around Sandtable Shoal, squalls without warning, and deep water no pilot calls empty ([[Sawek]]).

> [!narration] Arrival
> The strait opens ahead of you, wider than the chart promised, water clear enough that the sandy bottom shows through in pale ribbons. Off the cliff town a pier reaches into the anchorage, and hulls wait in line beside it with sails slack while clerks read their papers, the fort's guns covering the queue from the heights above. Under your keel the current leans from astern. The helmsman keeps his line without working for it. Spinner dolphins cross your bow in a long arc, sounding and rising, and beyond them the merchant lane runs on into the strait like a road.

## Play

### Travel

The prevailing winds blow across the whole Scatter, and eastbound ships run with wind and current both. Westbound ships beat for the full length, tacking between the shores, which is how pilots develop opinions about which tacks are less bad. Fog forms in the eastern strait overnight and burns off by mid-morning, more reliably in summer. Rain squalls move fast and give little warning, though the spinner dolphins tend to sound before one arrives.

Four ways to run it, and the choice is real:

- **The Blue Lane.** The deep mid-strait road that follows the strongest line of the current, roughly equidistant between the shores. It is not a marked channel. It is where every merchant sails because every merchant sailed there before, and it is the fastest water with the most room under the keel. It is also the most watched: a hull in the Lane is visible to both shores, to the patrols and to the Tidefall clerks.
- **The shore lees.** The northern and southern reef approaches. Bottom friction strips the current's strength, so the run is slower, and the lee pockets under the Crown coasts and the Midchain's northern reef faces give shelter and less attention. The cost is time, reefs and a pilot who knows the pockets.
- **Skirting the Gate.** The northern reef gaps that cross into Crown water without presenting papers at the Tidefall inspection. The Crown patrols know exactly which gaps are in use and have formed opinions about the hulls that use them. The cost is capture, or a payment at the western mouth before departure.
- **The Kalowe Run.** The angled cut from the Lane south through the northern Midchain reef approaches to [[Kalowe]]. No chart carries it: the waypoints pass pilot to pilot. The cost is a pilot's fee or a bought set of waypoints.

Two readings decide the eastern end:

- **Holding the lane in fog.** Overnight fog in the eastern strait leaves sound and water colour to carry the course. Wisdom (Survival) DC 12 for the watch. A failure ends the watch outside the Lane, in the water where the current begins to turn.
- **The Cold Turn.** At the eastern end the current bends away north along the Maw's western edge. Pilots call the turn the Cold Turn and a missed turn the Fool's Assist. Reading the water in time is Wisdom (Survival) DC 12. A failure rides the ship north toward the [[Stenmark]] cliffs, and what getting clear costs depends on how far it runs before the error shows. The water speaks first, the blue-green going a shade deeper. The lead loses the bottom next. The [[High Eyrie]] stack confirms the position pilots call the Eyrie Light, though no light burns there.

Rest and supply:

- **The Calven Roads.** Broad sheltered water south of [[Calven and Calveno|Calven]], where the island mass blocks the prevailing wind. The best anchorage in the northern strait for a ship waiting out weather or tide.
- **Harwick Reach.** The deep water south of [[Harwick]], where patrol frigates stage. Rest here is Crown-supervised and needs papers.
- **[[Kalowe]].** Supply, repairs, pilots, and a shrine that charges every hull.

### Rumors

- "Papers for three ports and an answer to none of them." Kalowe harbour talk, naming the [[Bad Receipt]]. True: a Kalowe-refit schooner carrying enough false registry to make a lawful captain hesitate. Investigate: set her entered registry at the Tidefall pier against the Kalowe yard's refit book.
- "Miss the turn and the current carries you to Stenmark's cliffs." Pilots say it about the eastern end. True: the Cold Turn bends the current north along the Maw's edge, and missed turns get called the Fool's Assist. Investigate: buy an eastbound pilot's briefing, or watch the deep water go a shade darker and the Eyrie bearing hold through one fog morning.
- "Glass Debt is sighted, and by evening the convoys close up." Merchants say it when the convoy boards go up. True: a report of [[Glass Debt]] or [[Velvet Noose]] closes the convoys and changes the patrol schedule, and the changed schedule tells you who believed the report. Investigate: compare convoy assembly times against the patrol rosters Kalowe's shrine board shows.

## Depth

### History

The inspection regime dates to 1195 DR, when the Dravosi Crown occupied the [[Crown Islands]] and founded the port at the western mouth ([[Campaign Timeline]]). The Blue Lane is older than any chart of it. The road exists because every merchant sailed where the merchants before them sailed, and the pilots who gave the Cold Turn and the Fool's Assist their names learned it the same way.

### Hidden truths

- **The Gate is an intelligence post.** Inspection at Tidefall is a tax post and an information post rather than customs. Timing, paperwork quality and who was paid at the western mouth before departure decide the crossing. The pier clerks' questions tell you which cargo the Crown wants named.
- **Convoy timing is political information.** Schedules tighten when pirate reports arrive, and the tightening leaks decisions worth money to whoever reads them first.
- **The patrols hold the gaps.** Every northern reef gap used to skirt the Gate is known to the Crown, and that knowledge shapes the patrol pattern. The next gap to get a visit is the one a smuggler should not use.
- **Sandtable's salvage is a grudge.** The Crown salvage contract for groundings on Sandtable Shoal is held by a [[Kalowe]] operator, and the [[Harwick]] Admiralty considers the arrangement an embarrassment. An embarrassment that size is leverage for whoever can move it.

### Threads

[[The Crown Inspection]] keeps the Crown's pursuit on this water, with [[Rupert Knighton]]'s squadron working the strait. [[Drowned Maw Awakening]] owns the eastern end, where the current turns and the water stops matching the charts.
