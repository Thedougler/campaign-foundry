## Tone

Dark heroic fantasy. Heroism costs. Victories are real and they leave a mark.

The Shattered Sea's own register: swashbuckling polish complicated by pettiness. Capable people make real danger, and vanity, resentment and immature needs wreck their best work. High competence sits inside low emotional maturity.

Build each major character from one public image, one real competence, one humiliating need and one petty fixation. Build each major scene from one serious external threat and one petty internal conflict: a boarding action that pauses over bad gunpowder, a treaty that fails on seating and décor. Banter is social combat, with interruption, precision insults, status testing and old slights never forgotten.

Keep glamour, danger, decadence and sharp dialogue, with consequences that stick. Comedy comes from the characters' flaws. The sea and its monsters can still kill, and the people are the disasters. Never give two NPCs the same sarcastic voice, and never let a joke erase a consequence. Skip broad clown comedy, parody and non-stop one-liners.

NPC dialogue defaults to a register that tests status and guards real needs. Expect competent professionals undone by interpersonal baggage.

This guidance governs narrative style. Lines and Veils sets hard content boundaries. Tone never overrides them, accessibility, safety or an owner page's established truth.

## Themes

- Rebellion against authority.
- Grey morality. Every side has dirt on it.
- Freedom from slavery, whether chains, debt, papers, or a patron's claim.
- Power corrupts. Those who hold it, and those who seize it.

## Lines and Veils

A line never appears in any form: not shown, referenced, implied or carried as backstory or consequence. A veil may happen in the fiction, but the telling cuts away before the act. One sentence acknowledges it. When in doubt, treat a topic as a line.

- **Line: self-harm.** No depiction, implication or consequence of self-inflicted harm.
- **Veil: hanging.** An execution or death by hanging may be named as an outcome. Cut before the act and never describe it.
- **Veil: sex.** It may be implied or acknowledged as having happened. It is never depicted, described or played out in scene.
