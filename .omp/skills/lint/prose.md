# Prose fixes

Read by whoever fixes lint's `narration`, `style` and `boilerplate` findings: the lint run itself, or a subagent given a page batch. The page check is the **checker**. It reports each rule a draft matches, with each echo's source and line. Check each draft at once and fix what the checker reports. A callout written fresh for a new stub page is judged the same way, pairing against the Canon facts it is drawn from.

Each rewrite is judged as a **pair**: the block as it stood when you started, and your rewrite. A pair passes when all of these hold:

- **No drift.** The rewrite asserts exactly what the original did: every event, name, count, cause, owner and the meaning of each quoted line, nothing added, dropped, merged, inverted or re-attributed. "Grey Crown hulls" keeps "Crown". Aho and Felix stay two people. A ledger "promised as safer than an answer" stays a promise. "The Pearl of Souls" is never given an owner.
- **Names stay names.** A Canon name (a page name or alias) stays as written. Players know these names, and the checker skips them when it counts. A recap uses the Party's own names for its people, ships and gods.
- **Plain speech.** English word order with the adjective before its noun: "silver scales", "clay bowls", "gold and green grass". When a rule matches, say the fact a different way in plain speech.
- **Aloud.** The DM could say the rewrite to a table of Players as idiomatic English, the words a fluent speaker would use unprompted. It sounds at least as good as the original.
- **Presence.** Say what a thing is, has or does ("the crater is silent", "his hands are empty").
- **Every page is its own page.** Draw each callout and bullet from this page's own facts. Its sentences then belong to this page alone. A recast shared line states this page's fact where the shared sentence stood.

Fix each finding in the page's wording. Warnings count, and so do played Session records and heard Narration. A finding that looks like an obvious false match on literal campaign meaning (a ship that is literally a ship) is rewritten too. Report it in your return for the DM to confirm, as `AGENTS.md` **Zero findings** sets out. Template wording is the one exception, and the `boilerplate/shared-line` rule under [Gate rules](#gate-rules) sets it out. A rule message that advises disabling the rule is addressed to the DM, since only the DM switches rules off. Its finding binds you, and a literal-meaning case goes in your return as a suspected false match. A sentence an earlier edit broke ("at the Drowned The Maw matters", "His left leg of is splinted") is repaired until its pair passes. Quoted lines keep their words wherever no finding touches them. A finding inside one rewords it to say the same thing. A `dialogue-attribution` finding moves the speaker's tag ahead of the line with the quoted words unchanged. Where theatre-of-the-mind differs, these rules govern under lint. That covers its step 5 File and its warning-as-judgement clause. It also covers its word-for-word heard speech.

## Steps

1. **Gather.** Load `theatre-of-the-mind` once for the whole batch. For each page, read the page and its findings, and the lines each `echo` or `shared-line` finding cites on other pages. For each flagged block (a callout, or the paragraph holding a flagged sentence) write its **ledger**: the facts it asserts (names, events, counts, causes, owners, quoted meanings). **Done when** every flagged block has its ledger and every line the findings cite is in context.
2. **Recast.** Write each flagged block anew from its ledger, a callout through theatre-of-the-mind steps 1 to 4 with its title kept. A **recast** composes each sentence fresh from the facts, with a new subject, verb and order, as an editor recasts a sentence. One recast clears every rule on the block at once. Swapping one word leaves the old sentence's structure in place, and the neighbouring rule matches that structure. Write prose in British spelling ("sabre", "rowing boat"). A statblock fence is exempt, and its US forms stay inside it. **Done when** every flagged block has a recast with every ledger fact in it.
3. **Check.** Save one page's recasts, then check that page alone: `bun run cf -- check "<page>"`. Each finding sends its whole sentence back to step 2: recast it from its ledger toward the passing form its `fix:` line names. An echo that cites a source you have not read sends you to those lines first. For a rule new to you, read `.vale/styles/<Style>/<Rule>.yml`. Rerun after each round of fixes, and finish the page before you check the next one. **Done when** the check prints `ok: 0 findings` for each batch page.
4. **Proof.** For each pair, tick each ledger fact in the rewrite. A fact the rewrite adds fails as surely as one it drops. Then read the rewrite aloud on its own, as the DM would say it to the table. Read it again beside the original and test it against every pair test above. A clean check is the floor and the pair tests are the bar: recast any failing pair through steps 2 and 3. **Done when** every ledger ticks, every pair passes, and each page's last check is still clean.
5. **Return.** List each touched page path with its pairs, before then after, and each suspected false match with its rule and sentence. A review that sends pairs back gets the sentences it cites recast through steps 2 to 4, with the new pairs returned. Leave `bun run cf -- check --fix`, the log and the slice's page gate to the lint run. **Done when** every touched page and every pair is listed.

## Gate rules

Consult this while drafting. The checker has the last word. Each finding's `fix:` line gives its passing form, and the rule's definition is the place to look when that line leaves you unsure.

Wikilinks read as their shown text. Page names and aliases, bare or linked, are masked before the `Narration.*` and `ai-tells.*` styles run, and the wording rules skip a page's own name. A page of a concrete kind reads as its kind's noun (`Person` for an NPC, PC, Deity or Creature, `People` for a Faction, `Ship` for a Vehicle, `Object` for an Item, `Place` for a Location). Any other page reads as a made-up `Placename…` word. The finding quotes that stand-in: `'The Ship carried'` quotes a sentence where a Vehicle's name is the subject of `carried`. A finding on the stand-in applies to the plain-English sentence too and binds like any other.

- **`narration/*`** (warnings, every `[!narration]` callout): `echo`, `fresh-starts`, `spoken-word-trap`, `evaluative-stack`, `relative-chain`, `invented-names` and `dialogue-attribution`, defined in `src/narration/analyze.ts`. "Outside quotes" means the text left once every `"…"` / `“…”` line is cut out. `echo` compares each callout with the rest of its page, frontmatter values included, and with each page the callout links.
- **`boilerplate/shared-line`** (warnings): the same sentence word for word on two pages, defined in `src/check/layers/boilerplate.ts`. The finding cites each sharer's path and line. A sentence shared inside otherwise different paragraphs counts too. Each sharer recasts its own line. The recast says, in place of the shared sentence, what this page alone records on that point, at about the shared sentence's length. Find it in the page's other sections. A field still states its own subject: a **Weak to** line gives an action that works against this creature, and a **Threat** line gives this creature's CR and what it does to this Party. When two sharers of one sentence are both in your batch, recast each from its own page, and each recast then differs from the other as well as from the original. A template's own words stay as the template gives them: its field labels, and a Recap Thread line's status word (moved, opened, resolved or still). Recast the sentence beside them. A finding that flags the template's wording alone keeps those words and goes in your return as a suspected false match.
- **`Narration.*`** (errors, callouts): theatre-of-the-mind cites each rule beside its passing form. The YAML is in `.vale/styles/Narration/`.
- **`ai-tells.*`** (errors, all prose on the page): the YAML is in `.vale/styles/ai-tells/`. These rules read sentence shapes. Draft the recurring ones in their passing form:
  - **Things and roles** (`ai-tells.Figurative*`, `ai-tells.BareHolds`, `ai-tells.BareNames`, `ai-tells.BareReaches`). A thing, a place or an unnamed role ("the reef", "the captain") is, has or contains something, or lies somewhere. Verbs of a mind, a hand or a life belong to people. A creature stalks or preys on, and a slain one was killed.
  - **Absence** (`ai-tells.NegatedObject`, `ai-tells.NegatedSubject`, `ai-tells.NegatedPair`, `ai-tells.ContrastiveNegation`). Negate one verb once with "doesn't", opening on a pronoun or joined to the sentence before. Better still, state what is there ("the steel is unmarked").
  - **Counts and series** (`ai-tells.CataphoricForecasting`, `ai-tells.VerbTricolon`). Put a count after its verb ("the Fleet lost five ships"). Give a series two items or four, or split the sentence.
  - **Clauses** (`ai-tells.SemicolonUsage`). End a complete clause with a full stop, or join it to the next with a comma and "and", "but" or "so".
  - **Also frequent:** `ai-tells.ContrastiveFormulas`, `ai-tells.StrawmanContrast`, `ai-tells.MicDrop`, `ai-tells.ConsequenceClause`, `ai-tells.FigurativeShape`, `ai-tells.FigurativeNouns`, `ai-tells.AnthropomorphicJustification` and `ai-tells.FillerIntensifier`. The `grammar` layer flags `every one`.
