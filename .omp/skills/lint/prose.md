# Prose fixes

Read by whoever fixes `narration`, `style` and `boilerplate` findings once the checker has reported them. That is the lint run or one of its subagents, or any writer repairing its own page (root `AGENTS.md` **Write, then repair**). The page check is the **checker**. It reports each rule a draft matches, with each echo's source and line. Check each draft at once and fix what the checker reports. A callout written fresh for a new stub page is judged the same way, pairing against the Canon facts it is drawn from.

Each rewrite is judged as a **pair**: the block as it stood when you started, and your rewrite. A pair passes when all of these hold:

- **No drift.** The rewrite asserts exactly its ledger: every event, name, count, cause, owner and the meaning of each quoted line, nothing added, dropped, merged, inverted or re-attributed. "Grey Crown hulls" keeps "Crown". Aho and Felix stay two people. A ledger "promised as safer than an answer" stays a promise. "The Pearl of Souls" is never given an owner. A rhythm fix splits, joins or reorders the block's own facts. A vague word ("huge") gives way to a plainer word or goes, and any sentence or measure the ledger lacks ("bigger than the cutter") is drift.
- **Names stay names.** A Canon name (a page name or alias) stays as written. Players know these names, and the checker skips them when it counts. A recap uses the Party's own names for its people, ships and gods.
- **Plain speech.** English word order with the adjective before its noun: "silver scales", "clay bowls", "gold and green grass". When a rule matches, say the fact a different way in plain speech.
- **Print grade.** The rewrite meets root `AGENTS.md` **Print grade** when the DM says it aloud to a table. A line that merely passes the gate fails, even where the original was no better: "Dust settles over the cabin. It leaves the seat clean.", "Cloth and wax bed it deep.", "Fur and folded wings pack it close from wall to wall.", "After a cannon went onto your vessel's account".
- **Presence.** Say what a thing is, has or does ("the crater is silent", "his hands are empty").
- **Every page is its own page.** Draw each callout and bullet from this page's own facts. Its sentences then belong to this page alone. A recast shared line states this page's fact where the shared sentence stood, at about its length, and recasts of one shared sentence in one batch differ from each other too. Each field still states its own subject: a **Weak to** line gives an action that works against this creature, and a **Threat** line gives this creature's CR and what it does to this Party.

Fix each finding in the page's wording as root `AGENTS.md` **Zero findings** sets out, played Session records and heard Narration included, and put each suspected false match in your return for the DM to confirm. Template wording is the one exception. A template's field labels and a Recap Thread line's status word (moved, opened, resolved or still) stay as the template gives them, and you recast the sentence beside them. A finding on that wording alone goes in your return as a suspected false match. A rule message that advises disabling the rule is addressed to the DM, since only the DM switches rules off. Its finding binds you, and a literal-meaning case goes in your return as a suspected false match. A sentence an earlier edit broke ("at the Drowned The Maw matters", "His left leg of is splinted") is repaired until its pair passes. Quoted lines keep their words wherever no finding touches them. A finding inside one rewords it to say the same thing. Moving a speaker's tag ahead of a line keeps the quoted words unchanged. Where theatre-of-the-mind differs, these rules govern under lint. That covers its step 5 File and its warning-as-judgement clause. It also covers its word-for-word heard speech.

## Steps

1. **Gather.** Load `theatre-of-the-mind` once for the whole batch. For each page, read the page and its findings, and the lines each `echo` or `shared-line` finding cites on other pages. A plain `read` cuts a long line short, so read a flagged one-line callout whole with a `:raw` range (`<page>:<line>-<line>:raw`) and later replace that whole line. For each flagged block (a callout, or the paragraph holding a flagged sentence) write its **ledger**: the facts it asserts (names, events, counts, causes, owners, quoted meanings). When a recast uses facts from the page's other sections, its ledger holds them too. A callout's ledger takes the facts theatre-of-the-mind step 2 gathers for its slot, after the `flesh-out` pass that step runs on a thin page. For a shared line, the ledger takes this page's own fact on its point. **Done when** every flagged block has its ledger and every line the findings cite is in context.
2. **Recast.** Write each flagged block anew from its ledger, a callout through theatre-of-the-mind steps 1 to 4 with its title kept, reading the exemplar passages in repo-root `reference/gm-voice/exemplars.md` (theatre-of-the-mind step 2) only to fix a `voice-envelope` finding. A **recast** composes each sentence fresh from the facts, with a new subject, verb and order, as an editor recasts a sentence. Swapping one word leaves the old sentence's structure in place, and the neighbouring rule matches that structure. Draft every new sentence in the [Gate rules](#gate-rules) forms before the first check. Statblock fences keep their US spellings. **Done when** every flagged block has a recast with every ledger fact in it, each sentence written in the Gate rules forms.
3. **Check.** Save one page's recasts, then read that page's own check (`skill://lint` § Commands, **Page check**). The check is the measuring tool and runs in about two seconds, so loop draft, check, adjust, and take every count, mean and spread from its finding text alone. Each finding sends its whole sentence back to step 2: recast it from its ledger toward the passing form its message and `fix:` line name. A finding quotes a page name or alias as a stand-in: its kind's noun (`Person` for an NPC, PC, Deity or Creature, `People` for a Faction, `Ship` for a Vehicle, `Object` for an Item, `Place` for a Location) or a made-up `Placename…` word for any other page. `'The Ship carried'` then quotes a sentence with a Vehicle's name as its subject, and the finding binds that sentence like any other. An echo that cites a source you have not read sends you to those lines first. For a rule new to you, read `.vale/styles/<Style>/<Rule>.yml`. Finish the page before you check the next one. **Done when** the check prints `ok: 0 findings` for each batch page, apart from findings that failed twice (`skill://lint` step 7).
4. **Proof.** For each pair, tick each ledger fact in the rewrite. A fact the ledger lacks fails as surely as one the rewrite drops. Then read the rewrite aloud on its own, as the DM would say it to the table. Read it again beside the original and test it against every pair test above. A clean check is the floor and the pair tests are the bar: recast any failing pair through steps 2 and 3. **Done when** every ledger ticks, every pair passes, and each page's last check still meets step 3's criterion.
5. **Return.** List each touched page path with its pairs, before then after, each finding that failed twice, quoted with its page, and each suspected false match with its rule and sentence. A review that sends pairs back gets the sentences it cites recast through steps 2 to 4, with the new pairs returned. Leave `--fix`, the log and the slice's page gate to the lint run. **Done when** every touched page and every pair is listed.

## Gate rules

The passing forms for the findings you repair. Recast each flagged sentence into the form its rule names.

- **People act.** A thing, a place or an unnamed role ("the reef", "the captain") is, has or contains something, or lies somewhere. A name or a pronoun takes the verbs of a mind, a hand or a life ("Malone asks", "he decides"). A creature stalks or preys on, and a slain one was killed.
- **Presence.** State what is there ("the steel is unmarked"). Where a negation is the fact, negate one verb once with "doesn't", opening on a pronoun or joined to the sentence before.
- **Counts and series.** Put a count after its verb ("the Fleet lost five ships"), and open a list on its first item. Give a series two items or four, adjectives and comma-joined phrases included, or split it into sentences. Where a template or skill asks for three things, give each its own sentence or bullet.
- **Clauses.** End a complete clause with a full stop, or join it to the next with a comma and "and" or "but". After "so", open on a pronoun ("so it holds") or follow the new subject with "is" or a modal ("so the deck is clear"). Join a thing to what it is with "is".
- **Direct statement.** State each fact once, in literal words, with its doer before an active verb. Give each modifier as a measured fact ("thirty feet off").
- **Own words.** Draw each sentence from this page's own facts, with Canon names as written. Write in British spelling ("sabre", "rowing boat").
