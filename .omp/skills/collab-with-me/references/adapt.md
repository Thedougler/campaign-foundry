# Adaptation

Adaptation turns a Story into material the table plays. A Story authors events; a campaign authors situations, the tools the Party can use on them and the questions play will answer. Every fiction technique carries over once its unit of authorship moves from events to those situations, tools and open questions, while the Players, their choices and the dice generate the events ([story-vs-campaign.md](../../../../docs/research/creative-writing/story-vs-campaign.md) §3). The failure this guards against is Alexander's railroad (§1):

```text
"Railroads happen when the GM negates a player's choice in order to enforce a
preconceived outcome."
```

Convert every bible section through the row that names it. Each owner defines the rule for its campaign form. Read the owner and apply it there.

## Steps

1. **Convert.** For each table row that matches a bible section, write the adapted form under the idea in the notes, as a pitch the DM keeps or drops, and set the idea's `Stage` to `adapt`. Done when every bible section has an adapted form in the notes.
2. **Test for the railroad.** Read each adapted item as the Party would meet it. Ask what happens when the Party chooses differently from the Story, whether it refuses or arrives late, sides with the antagonist or never shows up. Rework each item that works for one choice alone until every choice leaves it playable. Done when no adapted item requires a specific PC choice.
