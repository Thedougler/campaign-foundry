# The Mortis

> *A Mortis is never a punishment. It is a fact about who the character is, expressed through mechanics.*

The Mortis is **optional**. You do not have to take one, and if you don't, nothing about your character changes.

If you do, here's the offer. You name something that limits your character — a fact they can't escape — and something that makes them who they are. The DM writes both into the rules. The **Mark** is the limitation; the **Gift** is the strength. They always describe the same person: your character isn't someone who happens to be cursed on top of who they are. Their weakness and their strength are two sides of one coin.

A Mortis pushes you further into the character you already want to play. The glass cannon gets glassier; the tank gets tankier. The Mark is not a tax the DM collects — it's a weakness *you* lean into. You'll play around it naturally, and playing around it *is* the roleplay. The Gift is a modest reward for committing to that fiction.

## The two sides

- **The Mark** — a weakness, written in standard rules: disadvantage, a lost proficiency. It should be a limitation you *want* your character to carry. You'll feel it most as the road you choose not to take.
- **The Gift** — a strength, written in standard rules: a +2 ability score, advantage, a proficiency. It targets the core of your character fantasy. The Gift is always worth a little more than the Mark — a reward for leaning in, not a price.

Ground rules:

- **One Mortis per character.**
- **It does not change as you level.** Through the first few sessions, either you or the DM may rebalance it once if it turns out miscalibrated; after that it's locked.
- **A +2 ability-score Gift cannot raise a score above 20.**
- **Standard rules only** — advantage, disadvantage, proficiencies, ability scores — so a Mortis fits in a line on your sheet and never slows play.

## Creating a Mortis

1. Name the flaw — a fact that limits your character in a specific, consistent way.
2. The DM turns it into a mechanical Mark.
3. The DM proposes a Gift that exceeds the Mark and targets your fantasy.
4. You both agree. It locks in. The DM has final say on both sides.
