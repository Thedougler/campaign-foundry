# Running the Mortis

Design and calibration notes for the DM. The player-facing offer lives in [[mortis|The Mortis]].

## What a Mortis is for

The Mortis is not a balance tool — it's a character-creation covenant. The Mark formalizes the weakness a player already wants their character to carry; the Gift formalizes the strength they lean into. The net effect is a modest positive, on purpose: it's the reward for committing to a real limitation, not a price for power. Nobody is worse off for declining. A player will play around their Mark — avoiding the situations it disadvantages — and that avoidance is the roleplay working as intended.

## The avoidable / forced split

A Mark's teeth come in two kinds:

- **Avoidable** — an ability *check*. The player chooses whether to attempt it, so they can play around it. Most Marks should be this kind.
- **Forced** — a *saving throw*. It's inflicted on the character; the player can't route around it. A forced Mark is harsher and justifies a larger Gift.

Size the Gift to the Mark: an avoidable Mark carries a small Gift (a +2 ASI, advantage on two skills); a forced Mark may carry a larger, even complex Gift.

## Complex Gifts

A complex Gift — triggered states, borrowed proficiencies, conditional expertise — is permitted **only** for a forced Mark, and **only** if every trigger is written down before play. It is the ceiling, not the template. The default Mortis is one line on the sheet.

## Calibration guardrails

- The Mark must be a real limitation of the character's *fantasy* — one the player will feel. It does not need to trigger often; it needs to matter when it does.
- The Gift must target the core fantasy — not a consolation prize.
- A Mark that punishes a core class feature is not a Mortis.
- A Mark requiring case-by-case adjudication is not a Mortis — unless it's a forced Mark with pre-written triggers.
- A +2 ability-score Gift cannot raise a score above 20.

## Renegotiation

The definition locks in and does not change as the character levels. Through roughly the first three sessions, either side may rebalance once if a Mark under-triggers or a Gift overshoots. After that, it is locked.

## The Party's Mortises

### The Small — [[perrin-black-jaw|Perrin Black-Jaw]] · avoidable Mark

Not built to haul rope or take a hit in a scrum — so he leads instead.

- **Mark:** Disadvantage on all Strength-based checks and saving throws.
- **Gift:** Charisma score increases by 2.

### The Unmasked — [[jean-claude-tabarnack|Jean-Claude Tabarnack]] · avoidable Mark

What he is reads clearly — in his face, in his stillness, in how he positions before a fight.

- **Mark:** Disadvantage on Charisma (Persuasion) and Charisma (Deception) checks.
- **Gift:** Advantage on Wisdom (Perception) and Wisdom (Survival) checks.

### The Crowded — [[delmar-fisk|Delmar Fisk]] · forced Mark, complex Gift

Five captains' souls pressed into him by Umberlee as the fleet sank.

- **Mark:** Disadvantage on all Wisdom saving throws. When a captain's trigger fires, that captain surfaces involuntarily.
- **Gift:** Once per short rest (bonus action), surface one captain. While surfaced, gain that captain's skill proficiencies as borrowed mastery; if already proficient, gain Expertise instead. The brass flagon returns Delmar to the surface from any captain.
