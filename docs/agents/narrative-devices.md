# Narrative devices

The shared craft reference for the devices that vary a Session's tone and tie one Session to the next. Each consuming skill's step names the section it applies: read that section when the step runs. At the table these devices mostly run backwards. The DM scatters details and watches which ones the Players pick up. Afterwards the DM ties those to the story.

## Planted details and connection

Sort each detail by its job.

- **Essential Clues.** A Clue the Party needs for progress keeps a fixed truth and three independent routes, as `prep-session` step 5 sets out.
- **Colour.** Optional colour details are left uncommitted, and play decides their meaning later.
- **Connection.** Between Sessions, read the last two Session Ledgers in `archive/*.ledger.md` for what the Players fixated on: `SAID` lines where a Player, in or out of character, asks a question or offers a theory, `PLAY` choices that chase a detail, and `MOMENT` lines. Connect each of those details to a live Thread. The Ledgers already record these fixations, so no template or Wiki field tracks them.

## Clue ladders

A slow reveal climbs in stages. It opens on unease and moves to physical evidence. Direct contact comes last. Write each stage as its own Clue, true and findable without the stages after it. Key the stages to different actions or senses. A second approach then climbs where the first one stopped. A divination or check refused an answer is a stage too: write what the refusal itself shows.

## Rumour tiers

Rumours come in DC 10, 15 and 20 tiers, each revealing a deeper part of the truth. Mix petty gossip into every tier so the true rumours look like the rest.

## Callbacks

Each Scene plants one detail for a later callback. It is a concrete thing the Party perceives, such as an emblem or a habit, that can return with a different meaning. Place it where the Party meets it, in a callout, a Cue or a Situation line. Record it in the row's planning note under Prep's Scene Chart with where it could return, or in the return of a Scene without Prep. Seen again in a new context it can pay off as a joke or as grief, and the Players decide which.

A lead the Party passed over returns as a World event: the plan behind it went ahead without them. Recurring NPCs come back.

## Contrast

Each Scene has one contrast beat: a light moment that does work, or a turn from one register to its opposite. Prep supplies it on a DM-side line where the Scene keeps such material, such as an Angles bullet, a bench line, a Cue or a Reaction, and the DM chooses whether and when to spend it. Pitch it to the Campaign's `campaign-config.md` § Tone.

- **Humour with information in it.** A comic speaker or situation holds a Clue or a fact about how the World works, such as a ferryman's grumbling about tolls that shows who controls the river. The Party learns while it laughs. The laugh then costs no table time.
- **The tonal pivot.** Keep a serious image ready to follow a light beat, such as an arrival or a threat, written as its callout or Cue. After a laugh clears the palate, the next image gets the table's full attention. The pivot also runs the other way, when a hidden favour or an unexpected mercy turns panic into awe.
- **Banter as status-testing.** Two NPCs who needle each other, or a newcomer whose skills overlap a PC's, test who stands where. Give the pair opposed stances, or give the newcomer the overlap, and let the Players take it from there.

## Motifs

Give each Faction or force a colour and an image, and use them wherever it appears. A broken motif is a signal: a patrol bearing the wrong emblem tells the Party something has changed.

## Dramatic irony

The Players know something their characters don't, through an earlier Clue or a consequence the PCs caused. Protect the reveal. Player-facing text leaves a Player's theory unconfirmed until play reveals the answer. Use a cut-away or flash-forward vignette only when the DM asks for one.

## Red herrings

A red herring enters a page only when the DM names it as one.

## Closing stinger

A Resolution's stinger ends the Session on a peak at least as high as the danger just overcome. It can end on an arrival, such as a new NPC or a new place. It can also end on a revelation of the next goal or a secret, or cut mid-effect just before initiative. The next Session's Hook resolves the stinger within its opening minutes. Where the Session needs to settle after its peak, it closes on a decompression coda in place of a stinger. The coda is a quiet exchange or a still image of the aftermath, opened out to its scale, and the next Hook owes it nothing. The stinger belongs to the Resolution, and a Cliffhanger stays the action Scene `CONTEXT.md` defines.
