# Scene pages

The common authoring procedure for `hook-scene`, `development-scene`, `cliffhanger-scene`, `climax-scene` and `resolution-scene`. Each skill supplies its kind's craft and card catalogue; this file owns their inputs, page contracts, conditional handoffs and completion. Read the kind's skill before designing.

## Steps

1. **Establish the assignment.** Follow `AGENTS.md` orientation and read `CONTEXT.md`, `docs/wiki-layout.md` and the current Scene template. Reuse a caller's completed orientation only when it covers this Campaign and request. Resolve the World, Campaign, Session, exact kind, destination path and completion owner. A `prep-session` child receives its Scene Chart row, Party, Opposition, Clues, Spotlight, predecessor alternatives and destination paths. For a direct DM request, retrieve the existing Prep and row; a bounded brief can supply the kind, premise, entry state, Threads, Spotlight and intended carry-forward. Add or update only that Scene's planning material, preserving the Session's objective. When no Prep exists, author the requested Scene from that bounded brief and link it from the Campaign overview rather than inventing an incomplete Prep. Missing Session intent goes to `plan-session`; assembling or changing the whole chart belongs to `prep-session`. **Done when** the Scene's purpose and inputs are grounded and either a composing caller or this standalone operation owns the final gate and log.
2. **Ground the entry.** Use `qmd` to discover and retrieve relevant Wiki pages: the last played Recap and final Scene for a Hook, the predecessor's prepared outcomes for later Scenes, Party Sheets and goals, linked Threads, Locations, NPCs, Factions, Creatures, Items and applicable House Rules. Played events establish facts; unplayed branches remain alternatives. Carry positions, knowledge, ownership, conditions, resources and any live clock that changes this Scene. Match each reused table-use fact to the actual owner passage: terrain costs, damage, checks, escape procedures and encounter state retain those sourced values and conditions. Read relevant linked owners before replacing a rule with local improvisation. Decide genuinely missing design facts consistently with Canon and the DM's intent; distinguish them from established events in the return. **Done when** each opening dependency and reused rule has a retrieved source passage or a stated missing-fact design decision, and every reachable predecessor branch has an entry state rather than an assumed victory.
3. **Choose the shape.** Select the kind's card against the entry state, Thread and requested purpose. Keep the choice in Prep's planning material, not extra Scene properties or spoken Narration; without Prep, include it in the return for later charting. Apply the kind's craft and the Scripting the Game rules below. If a row supplies a card, use it unless it conflicts with the established fiction; return the reason for a local correction. **Done when** the chosen card, pressure or turn, table-time allotment, Spotlight and next destination fit the exact kind and chart or bounded standalone brief.
4. **Resolve the cast.** Reuse the first fitting source in `AGENTS.md` order: Wiki, 2024 SRD through `dnd5e-srd-api`, official or existing published/homebrew material through the harness's web search and fetch tools, then novel design. Retrieve full facts, not search snippets. Create only missing owners through the matching skill: `npc-design`, `creature-design`, `location-design`, `item-design`, `faction-design`, `vehicle-design`, `spell-design` or `lore-design` as needed. Supply Canon, local purpose, Party facts, intended paths and enclosing operation; receive complete pages and touched paths before relying on them. An NPC's fighting statistics come from its linked Creature, not its identity page. Verify every archived-Raw path used as provenance against a real readable file; take its exact path from retrieved evidence rather than deriving it from an entity's name. External rule sources belong in a body attribution, not `sources`; when no archived Raw applies, use `sources: []`. **Done when** every needed named entity and rules owner exists, its links and provenance paths resolve, and each new page has a content link in from the Scene or another relevant page.
5. **Write the runnable page.** Fill `<vault>/templates/Scene - <Kind>.md` at `<Campaign>/Sessions/Session <N>/Session <N> - <Scene title>.md`. Keep every required property, the exact `##` headings at their original depth and in order, and every required callout type; remove template guidance and retain optional structure only where it has content. Optional subheadings organise content inside that spine rather than replacing required sections. Use `type: Scene`, the exact `kind`, a useful summary and verified archived-Raw provenance in `sources`. Link the page from Prep's Scene Chart when present and update the row's planning notes or Clue routes where the Scene changes them. Put all table-use facts on the page: who wants what, their means and responses, useful spatial relationships, pressure triggers, accessible choices and consequences. Each hidden truth has a concrete way to learn it. Rule uncertain actions with Ability (Skill), DC, action or time cost and both results; saves name the ability, DC and effects, contests name both sides and ties. Sensible actions without meaningful doubt simply work. Keep required conclusions discoverable through three independent routes across the chart or standalone Scene; a failed check changes cost or position rather than hiding the only way forward. Use the Encounters section below when physical danger is present. Creature statistics remain on their owner pages; the Scene exposes them through resolving `![[Creature#Statblock]]` section embeds. **Done when** the filed page matches the template's actual spine and the DM can adjudicate each offered action from its sourced rules and rendered owner embeds.
6. **Compose Narration.** Once the DM-side facts exist, invoke `theatre-of-the-mind` with each named callout, its Scene kind, actual viewpoint and entry condition, spatial facts and the owner/source pages it may read. Request callout-only authoring and explicitly defer child tooling to this operation's final gate. Preserve the template's title: Hook, Development, Cliffhanger and Climax use `Opening`; Resolution uses `Closing image`. The Narration skill's corresponding recipe owns construction, length and its final check. Put NPC or Creature entering callouts under Play or Encounter, titled with the subject's name; a major Revelation gets its own conditional callout. If preceding alternatives change what is visible, write the selection condition in DM notes outside each variant's callout and state which variant the DM reads. When an established result settles an unplayed cut of time or space, place a `Transition` immediately before the destination's opening or closing slot; unresolved travel choices stay in play. Check the filed prose against the Narration skill's applicable final-check items using its actual words, then against every entry that can select it; repair mismatched carriers, companions, possessions or positions. **Done when** every required spoken slot is filed, its applicable Narration checks have textual evidence, each branch's visible facts are true on its own condition, and live events stop where the Players can respond.
7. **Resolve exits.** For Hook, Development, Cliffhanger and Climax, fill the template's `## Outcomes`: each materially different result states its trigger, the concrete World change and a resolving destination link on the chart. Cover offered approaches, failure, withdrawal, waiting or refusal where they change the situation. Give the receiving Scene the resulting knowledge, positions, ownership, conditions, resources and clock state it needs; share a destination only when its entry supports every incoming result. A standalone Scene without a chart names its next pressure, trigger and carried state in the same rows, ready for charting, rather than inventing links to unwritten pages. Resolution instead holds its Climax-conditioned payoffs, reactions, rewards and next-Session carry-forward in `## Play` and `## Threads`, as its template requires. Proposed deaths, captures, rewards and Thread changes remain conditional Prep, not updates to current Canon. **Done when** each prepared result has a usable successor state or Session-ending carry-forward and every existing receiving page accounts for it; return pending entry variants to the caller for pages not yet authored.
8. **Cold read.** Read the Scene and render its linked embeds as the DM would with no sourcebooks or chat available. Run the kind's completion criteria, then trace each entry through its options to an exit: names resolve, rules and statistics are available, pressure advances on stated triggers, critical information has multiple routes, the Narration fits the entry, and no result depends on the Party accepting a predetermined answer. Compare the finished heading spine with the actual template, every copied provenance path with its file, each reused ruling with its owner passage, and Balance numbers with the `bun run cf -- encounter-budget` output and Party evidence. Correct mismatches in the Scene and its local Prep material; return any required chart recomputation to its owner. **Done when** every entry and exit can be run, all source and template comparisons agree, every cited table-use fact is filed, and the kind's criteria hold in the actual page.
9. **Close the operation.** Follow Completion below. **Done when** the full index/gate/log cycle is observed for standalone work, or the explicit composing caller has all artifacts, touched paths, conditional states and remaining findings needed for its single completion cycle. A child return is not a claim that Prep or Push has completed.

## Scripting the Game

Mike Pondsmith's *Scripting the Game* is the pacing method; it takes precedence over a loose pool of potential Scenes. A Session has one Hook first, alternating non-action Developments and physical-risk Cliffhangers in the middle, then one Climax and its Resolution. An action Hook starts the middle with a Development; a cerebral Hook starts it with a Cliffhanger. The final middle Scene can hand directly to the Climax. A compact Session with no middle Scenes hands Hook to Climax.

Use approximately thirty minutes per Scene and the actual chart's Minutes allocation. The Hook, Climax and Resolution reserve about ninety minutes together; middle Scenes fit the remaining Session length, not a quota. A kind's shorter contest or closing image can leave room elsewhere. In-world clocks name their own units separately from table minutes. An uncertain result changes the next Scene's entry; the chart orders pressure and opportunities, not guaranteed victories, accepted jobs or routes.

## Encounters

For a Cliffhanger or Climax with Creatures that can be fought, including a violent fallback to negotiation, and for an action Hook borrowing Cliffhanger craft, take 2024 XP numbers from `bun run cf -- encounter-budget`. The command classifies Creature spend; it does not choose the opposition or measure every danger.

1. **Budget.** Establish the participating PCs from current Sheets and incoming conditions, excluding anyone unable to participate and explaining that exclusion; use character level across classes. Select Low, Moderate or High to match the row and the kind's craft. Run `bun run cf -- encounter-budget --help`, then print that party's budgets:

   `bun run cf -- encounter-budget --levels <each participating PC level>`

   Apply sourced House Rules first. Use the CLI totals instead of fetching a table, recalling a row, or adding XP by hand. The CLI adds a +1 combat offset to each recorded level (this Party fights above the calculator); pass sheet levels and do not pre-add it. **Done when** the contributing PCs and levels match the entry and the CLI has printed that party's Low, Moderate and High totals.

2. **Spend.** For each opposing type, take `count`, CR and XP from its retrieved Wiki or 2024 SRD statblock (Canon, then `dnd5e-srd-api`, then published material). Pass them to the same command; do not use a creature lookup database:

   `bun run cf -- encounter-budget --levels <levels> --target <low|moderate|high> --creature "Name,CR,XP,count"`

   Repeat `--creature` per type. Equal to a band ceiling is that band; one XP over High is Beyond High. No 2014 monster-count multiplier, no Easy/Medium/Hard/Deadly thresholds. Read the printed band and any `Vs` delta; decide whether that spend is the right fight. When Canon fixes an over-budget force, keep the CLI's label, state the excess, and give playable avoidance, escape or objective routes rather than claiming a balanced fight or changing recorded statistics. State when reinforcements arrive and whether waves overlap; account for every distinct opponent that can participate, without counting the same Creature again merely because its tactics change. Separate mutually exclusive opposing forces so each possible fight has its own run. A duel uses the PCs actually fighting. **Done when** each comparison is the CLI's printed totals for that force and the page's description of danger matches the planned contest, including what XP does not cover.

3. **Expose the plan.** Copy the CLI's Encounter Balance lines under `### Balance` in a Cliffhanger's `## Encounter`; in a fighting Climax or Hook, use `### Encounter` under `## Play` with `#### Balance`. Name `bun run cf -- encounter-budget` as the budget source, the participating PCs, and the Creature pages supplying XP. Keep the current Scene template; do not add a foreign encounter write-up. Include quantities, deployment, battlefield features, opposition objectives, opening tactics, adaptations and break/escape conditions. Embed each fought Creature as `![[Creature#Statblock]]` at this Encounter and link it elsewhere; resolve named NPC combatants to their Creature owners. XP measures Creature spend against the Party budget only: state how terrain, surprise, allies, depleted resources, hazards and objectives change the actual danger. **Done when** the DM has the entire playable opposition, every XP figure traces to the CLI output and a retrieved statblock, and the page states what the XP comparison does not measure.

A creature-free chase or hazard has no monster XP to spend. Keep any template-required `## Encounter` and explain why XP balance does not apply. Calibrate its actual danger to the Party with sourced DCs, damage or conditions, action costs, duration, escape routes and resource demands; the kind's skill supplies the contest engine. A non-action Development or Resolution keeps its own kind rather than adding an unplanned fight.

## Completion

### Standalone

Review the installed CLI help before using commands:

```bash
bun run cf -- check --help
bun run cf -- index --help
bun run cf -- log --help
bun run cf -- encounter-budget --help
```

From the repo root, regenerate the generated index and run the full unified gate, applying mechanical fixes without changing Canon:

```bash
bun run cf -- index
bun run cf -- check --fix
bun run cf -- check
```

Treat gate findings as unfinished authoring. Classify each reported finding, apply available mechanical fixes, repair the remaining content while preserving Canon, and rerun the full unfiltered gate after the repairs. Grammar and style findings require sentence-level revision; listing them in the reply does not complete the work. Keep every layer enabled and continue until the unfiltered gate exits 0. Path or layer filters are diagnostic only, not completion evidence. For an assigned filesystem root, add `--vault "<wiki>" --root "<root>"` to index, check and log, and `--templates "<wiki>/templates"` to check.

Append the operation log only after the latest full, unfiltered gate has exited 0. A nonzero gate result keeps the operation unlogged and the repair loop active. The single completed entry lists every touched content page by its vault-relative path:

```bash
bun run cf -- log --world "<World>" --op create --title "Author Session <N> <Scene title>" --page "<World>/<Campaign>/Sessions/Session <N>/Session <N> - <Scene title>.md"
```

Repeat `--page` for Prep and all dependent pages actually changed. Direct single-Scene work outside an enclosing Prep uses `create`; a composing operation keeps its own operation. Generated `index.md` and `log.md` belong to the CLI. Single-Scene work preserves `hot.md` and played Session records; full Prep owns its hot rewrite and Push.

Return the Scene and dependency paths, substantive design decisions and their grounding, conditional carry-forward, and the observed gate/log result. Repairable page findings remain work in progress, not blockers. An unavailable prerequisite is a blocker only when failed tool or capability output establishes it and supported remedies have been exhausted; report that evidence and the exact missing prerequisite without logging the operation as complete. Complete when the page and all dependencies are runnable, the index is current, the full gate passed, and the log entry exists.

### Composed

When an explicit caller owns the final operation, return the authored Scene, owner pages, changed Prep material, every touched path, design decisions, conditional incoming/outgoing states, and any tooling findings. Give the caller the actual pending destination paths and entry variants, not name-only promises. The caller regenerates the index, repairs findings through the full gate loop above, records one entry for all touched pages only after an observed unfiltered exit 0, and performs its remaining hot/Push steps. Keep those duties with that caller rather than issuing a separate child log. Complete when every local craft and cold-read criterion holds and the caller has the full return needed to finish end to end; that handoff does not turn a failing gate into a completed operation.
