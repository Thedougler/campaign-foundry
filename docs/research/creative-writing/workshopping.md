# 1:1 workshopping: research behind `collab-with-me`

Evidence for the 1:1 workshop in [`docs/agents/co-writing.md`](../../agents/co-writing.md) and ADR 0046, gathered on 2026-10-12. It has two parts. One records the DM's own words from past workshopping sessions. The other records sourced practices from writers' rooms, improv, creative problem solving, critique and human-AI co-writing. Each item ends with its verdict: the `co-writing.md` section that adopts it, or the reason it stays out. The wider survey of group workshops is in the DM's Second Brain ("Brainstorming, Workshopping and Collaborative Story Writing (Research)", with its 1:1 section). [`eval-and-cowriting.md`](eval-and-cowriting.md) holds the earlier co-writing evidence on ownership, homogenisation and yes-and.

## The DM's sessions

A scout searched omp transcripts across all projects for collab, brainstorm, workshop, riff, pitch, plan-session, new-campaign and story bible, then read each cited DM turn in context. Relevant turns came from `01a124ec-e208-72ab-8eb7-14e28ee2ba04` (Session 13), `01a10a33-2b6b-7768-9803-5ee00480c4ad` (Shattered Sea themes), `01a0a2c1-eae0-714e-ad90-dad5985907e9` (the first brainstorming skill), `01a0a2f8-03fd-7630-bd58-12c48e548e3a` and `01a01913-1d04-7000-865d-98e86ad8e720`. Quotes keep the DM's spelling.

### How he wants to work

1. He asked for a skill that works "loosely and collaboratively turn by turn", in "not an intensive grilling interview" (`01a0a2c1… c761d31e`), then added that the agent "should add to the conversation, not simply mirror back to the user" (`… 1e64769d`). Verdict: adopted as Role, Drawing him out and the one-question rule.
2. His ideas arrive in multi-turn, additive dumps. He may give a long lore turn, then return with "once her death bloom was only the graves…" (`01a124ec… 723ffb0d`). He may add a correction too: "nah skarnn didn't take it from a living plant, hes fine" (`… 043de818`). Verdict: adopted in Drawing him out (combine each turn into the picture) and Talk (a line per fragment while he pours out).
3. His voice runs from short commands to all-caps excitement to long connected prose, which argues for replies sized to the live idea rather than a form. Verdict: adopted in Talk.

### What worked

4. A specific, well-connected pitch drew an immediate idea of his own: "OOOH I REALLY LIKE MAKING HINEWAI TREE THE ONE SIMONE USED TO TURN HER SKIN PARTIALLY GOLD" (`01a124ec… fb10cc19`). That the pitch caused the idea is the scout's inference. Verdict: adopted in Canon and pitches (specific, named, tied to a pressure) and Going wide (heighten what lights him up).
5. When he corrected the three bodies of Hinewai, "I dont want it to feel like different people, more like the same disjointed consciousness talking to itself" (`01a124ec… ee55fb05`), the agent rebuilt the whole model, and he answered "its good go" (`… eadbd652`). Verdict: adopted in Ownership (rebuild the whole idea around a correction).
6. He argues themes through particulars with names: Dravosi "might making right" and Tessarine "systems for passive authority", along with Grung caste magic (`01a10a33… be1fc2c0`). Verdict: adopted in Canon and pitches.

### Where it went wrong

7. An elegant thematic synthesis displaced his spine until he redirected: "rebellion against authority is the central theme" (`01a10a33… be1fc2c0`). Verdict: adopted in Ownership (his centre wins).
8. He bounded Aruhe as "a little side track for them to get a taste of my plans for the midchain" (`01a10a33… f5f77c3d`). Verdict: adopted in Ownership (develop at the scale he set).
9. The agent read Nona as a public commander. He corrected: "shes more like an undergorund community activist, think rebellions in kitchens overwhispers etc" (`01a10a33… 2ed855ca`). In Session 13: "what that isnt a thing those are two distinct species" (`01a124ec… ea20cc0c`). Verdict: adopted in Ownership (where his concept leaves something open in kind, pitch one reading and leave the choice to him).
10. After a six-agent fan-out he said "dont use subagents" (`01a01913… 667497cb`), and later "I dont like the hyper specifc extra agent" (`01a124ec… fdd6e55c`). Verdict: subagents run in the background only and serve the conversation. On 2026-10-12 the DM added that a fresh critic subagent is welcome when he wants a harder read (ADR 0046).
11. No inspected turn endorses a menu of generic options. Notes and filing also interleaved with the creative talk in Session 13. Both points are the scout's inference. Verdict: seeds dropped, notes kept silent and filing lazy.

## Sourced practices

These adapt group-room and design frameworks to a dyad, although the sources do not study a DM working with an AI.

### Pitching and story-breaking

12. Start from the dramatic engine and the creator's intent. Jane Espenson begins with the show's "genre and feel and point" and foregrounds "emotional turns" ([Espenson, pitching](https://janeespenson.com/archives/00000538.php)). Verdict: adopted in Going wide (the wants, cost and turn).
13. Pitch concrete story moves: tell the episode "briefly, pointing out each act break and exciting revelation" ([Espenson, pitching](https://janeespenson.com/archives/00000538.php)). Verdict: adopted in Going wide.
14. Break coarse beats before prose, and rebreak when an outline exposes a hole ([Espenson, story break](https://janeespenson.com/archives/00000085.php)). Verdict: kept in `collab-with-me` `references/story.md` (outline before prose).
15. A short pitch can "feel more like a conversation", and the writer's feedback turns it into a two-way exchange ([Espenson, pitching](https://janeespenson.com/archives/00000538.php)). Verdict: adopted in Talk.
16. Solve story mechanics before decoration. Espenson rates a showrunner by the ability to "solve story problems early in the process" ([Espenson, story break](https://janeespenson.com/archives/00000085.php)). Verdict: adopted in Narrowing.

### Improv and partner co-creation

17. Accept the offer underneath a proposal and build a specific consequence. Hoopla's core practice is to "accept and build on the offers" ([Hoopla](https://www.hooplaimpro.com/resource/blocked-vs-unblocked-scenes)). Verdict: adopted in Ownership (fixed points).
18. A useful "yes, but" names a specific cost and offers a playable path. This adapts Hoopla's practice, without establishing a Hoopla rule. Verdict: adopted in Critic (weak spot, cost, fix).
19. Heighten a pattern by escalation or contrast, as in Espenson's "funnelling" ([Espenson](https://janeespenson.com/archives/00000069.php)). Verdict: adopted in Going wide (heighten what lights him up).

### Going wide, narrowing and drawing out

20. "Divergent and Convergent Thinking Must be Balanced", and immediate judgement shuts down idea generation ([Creative Education Foundation](https://www.creativeeducationfoundation.org/what-is-cps/)). Verdict: adopted as Wide and narrow, with critique held to sinking costs while going wide.
21. Reframe the challenge as an open question before solving it ([CEF](https://www.creativeeducationfoundation.org/what-is-cps/), [Design Council Double Diamond](https://www.designcouncil.org.uk/resources/the-double-diamond/)). Verdict: incorporated into Drawing him out.
22. Ask for the creator's taste one focused question at a time: "What part of this do you want to protect?", "What's the version you'd hate?" ([Lerman CRP](https://lizlerman.com/critical-response-process/), [CEF](https://www.creativeeducationfoundation.org/what-is-cps/)). The sources recommend these questions but do not test them as prompts. Verdict: adopted in Drawing him out.
23. Offer a few contrasting pitches, not near-duplicates. Dramatron writers valued "more interpretations and artistic freedom" and cherry-picking ([Dramatron](https://arxiv.org/html/2209.14958)). Doshi & Hauser found more ideas lift novelty ([eval-and-cowriting.md](eval-and-cowriting.md) §1). Verdict: adopted in Going wide (two to four options that differ in kind).
24. Force a connection to an incongruous image or pressure when ideation stalls. This is an adaptation of CEF. Verdict: covered by Going wide (a different engine or register).

### Honest critique

25. Begin with specific impact: what was "evocative, interesting, exciting, and/or striking" ([Lerman CRP](https://lizlerman.com/critical-response-process/)). Verdict: adopted in Critic (open full notes on what lands).
26. Ask neutral questions before diagnosing, and request permission before offering opinions ([Lerman CRP](https://lizlerman.com/critical-response-process/)). Verdict: adapted. The DM asked for unprompted one-line notes. The permission step applies to full notes alone.
27. Diagnose the problem and its cost, and offer a fix as one option. Pixar's Braintrust is candour without authority, from a press account ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/3027135/inside-the-pixar-braintrust)). Verdict: adopted in Critic, with a fix on every note at the DM's request.
28. Maintain a critical stance under pushback. Models more readily endorse a user's follow-up counterargument, even casual feedback without a justification ([Kim & Khashabi 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.1222/)). Holding a note until a reason arrives is an inferred countermeasure. Verdict: adopted in Critic.
29. Make critique goal-directed: exploration, stress-testing or notes on one weak spot. Dramatron output had logical gaps and lacked subtext ([Dramatron](https://arxiv.org/html/2209.14958)). Verdict: adopted as the fresh critic subagent in `collab-with-me` `references/critique.md`.

### Human-AI co-writing

30. Generate at the right level of abstraction and let the human intervene at any stage ([Dramatron](https://arxiv.org/html/2209.14958)). Verdict: kept in `references/story.md` (the DM edits at bible and outline level).
31. Treat AI output as sparks and raw material, not finished writing ([Dramatron](https://arxiv.org/html/2209.14958)). Verdict: adopted in Canon and pitches (a pitch becomes Canon when the DM keeps it).
32. Limit output to a size the human can react to. Wordcraft's 25 hobbyist writers reported engagement, though the study validates no response length ([Wordcraft](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3490099.3511105)). Verdict: adopted in Talk.
33. Human taste and continuity are the control layer ([Dramatron](https://arxiv.org/html/2209.14958)). Verdict: adopted in Ownership.

## Left out

- A full writers'-room process. A dyad has no staff, calendar or showrunner hierarchy.
- A fixed agenda, ledger, stage-gate or compulsory record. The sources establish none as useful overhead for an ongoing conversation.
- Yes-and as unconditional agreement. It preserves offers while going wide and leaves critique and continuity checks in place.
- Braintrust as permission to be harsh. Candour serves the work.
- AI output as a finished scene or verdict.
- The example questions as validated interventions.
- Claims about Brennan Lee Mulligan's or Dimension 20's preparation. No first-party source was specific enough to quote.
