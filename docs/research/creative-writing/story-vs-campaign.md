**Justin Alexander, "Don't Prep Plots"**
```
"If you're GMing a roleplaying game, you should never prep a plot."
"Your gaming session is not a story — it is a happening. It is something about
```

## 2. Comparison table

| Dimension | Where story & campaign craft agree | Where campaign craft diverges |
|---|---|---|
| Authorship of protagonists | Both build vivid POV characters | PCs belong to players; GM authors world and NPCs only ("not a frustrated novelist"; Ryan's internal mode) |
| Plot vs situation | Both want causally linked events (Ryan's narrative definition) | Prep situations/nodes, never event sequences; events emerge ("it is a happening") |
| Foreshadowing & payoff | Plant early info for later payoff (Laws' "pipe" beat; esoteric clues) | Outcome uncertain ⇒ redundancy: 3 clues per conclusion; secrets stored abstract from discovery site |
| Pacing | Both need tension rise/fall, up & down beats (HHP; Lazy DM) | The table controls pace: outline "potential scenes" you'll discard; DMG One-Hour Guideline |
| Character arcs | Both reward change (Aylett: characters "designed for change") | Arcs belong to players; GM only offers material (character secrets, session-zero ties, "Be a fan") |
| NPC knowledge/agency | Track what every character knows | NPCs act offscreen between sessions: grim portents advance regardless; "Ask what the villains and NPCs are doing right now" |
| Spoken vs read prose | Clarity and voice matter to both | GM prose is one-take spoken synthesis; hearable, not rereadable; boxed read-aloud minimal (Angry GM) |
| Reusable modular prep | Outlines organize material | Prep must be order-independent modules: locations, secrets, NPCs, definite/possible/unlikely encounters (nodes, not chapters) |
| Endings | Both want satisfying resolution | Endings cannot be predetermined; only stakes questions, "played to find out"; threats get impending dooms, not scripted finales |
| Spotlight sharing | POV rationing (chapters) | Spotlight rationed across live players; engineer each player type/trait a payoff (Laws; DMG "Know Your Players") |
| Information economy | Withhold to create mystery | Inverse: over-deliver clues ("more clues are always better"), permissive clue-finding, red herrings de-risked by redundancy |
| Villains/fronts offscreen | Antagonists need offscreen logic | Fronts codify villain plans as tickable grim portents + impulses; they act whether or not PCs intervene ("Think offscreen, too") |
| "Story = what happened at the table" | Story is the finished artifact | The a priori plot is "only hypothetical"; the story is the retrospective account of play (Alexander, Laws, Louchart) |

## 3. Adapting each fiction-technique family

- **Hierarchical outlining (logline → plot → scenes)** (Dramatron-style): keep logline (campaign pitch/six truths) and scene level (fantastic locations, keyed rooms), **delete the plot level** — replace with fronts (dangers + impulses + grim portents + stakes questions) and node maps of situations. Blank nodes are load-bearing ("Draw maps, leave blanks").
- **Beat sheets**: keep Laws' beat vocabulary as a *pacing checklist and post-session analysis tool*, not a script: sessions should "alternate upward and downward emotional beats"; write anticipation beats only when players can perceive the tell; attach beats to situations, so any ordering can carry them.
- **Multi-agent character simulation** (Agents' Room / character-agents): simulate NPCs and factions with goals and impulses ("What is the bad guy trying to do?"), never PCs. Use runs to generate grim portents, offscreen moves, and to detect **dead ends** (states where content is missing) and **density/critical mass** failures — exactly Louchart's authoring QA.
- **Candidate generation & reranking** (Re3/DOC): generate many candidate clues, locations, complications; rank by (a) clue redundancy satisfied (≥3 paths per conclusion), (b) abstraction from place of discovery, (c) discovery-independence, (d) Baker's test — does it make you curious what the characters will make of it?
- **Rewrite/revision passes**: revision happens in **prep against the situation**, never against the table's past — the session transcript is immutable ("one take"). Maintain revelation lists as checklists and revise by adding clues/tools, not by editing outcomes.
- **Voice-sample conditioning**: safe for recurring NPC speech, but condition on short spoken tags/diction, not literary prose style; output must survive one-take oral delivery; do not generate read-aloud blocks to be recited verbatim.
- **Context assembly**: assemble the runtime context from situation nodes + front state + revelation ledger + PC-driven facts, never from a linear manuscript. Track two knowledge states (what players know vs what the world knows); feed NPC knowledge forward between sessions so fronts advance offscreen.

**Net rule**: every fiction technique survives conversion by shifting the unit of authorship from *events* to *situations, tools, and open questions* — the table, dice, and players remain the event-generating process; the system prep supplies a dense, robust, reusable landscape for them to traverse.
